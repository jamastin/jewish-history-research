Hitler & the Other Figures

Each man: his key document, and one thing the newspapers said about him at the time.

Adolf Hitler

His writings: Mein Kampf (Vol. 1, July 18, 1925; Vol. 2, December 11, 1926) — the original German is free in the Institut für Zeitgeschichte critical edition, and the 1939 James Murphy English translation is free at Project Gutenberg Australia. About 1,200 volumes of his personal library survive at the Library of Congress.

In the papers: the Wisconsin Rapids Daily Tribune, January 30, 1933, front page: “HITLER NAMED NEW CHANCELLOR OF GERMANY.” See the scanned front page →

Heinrich Himmler

Reichsführer-SS; ran the SS and police empire that carried out the genocide. Key document: the Posen speech, October 4, 1943, where he told his SS generals, in a closed room, of “the evacuation of the Jews, the extermination of the Jewish people.” Read the speech (GHDI) →

In the papers: the Detroit Jewish Chronicle, June 1, 1945, after his suicide in British custody: he “will go down in history as the greatest murderer who ever lived.” Scanned page →

Reinhard Heydrich

Himmler's deputy; head of the Reich Security Main Office. Key document: the Wannsee Protocol, January 20, 1942 — the meeting he chaired, with its country-by-country table of some 11 million Jews. Read the protocol (GHDI) →

In the papers: an American AP report, June 4, 1942: “Reinhard Heydrich… died in Prague this morning of wounds suffered in an attempt on his life May 27.” USHMM, History Unfolded →

Adolf Eichmann

The deportation-logistics chief (RSHA Section IV B 4). Key record: his 1961 Jerusalem trial — 114 sessions, thousands of documents, held by the Israel State Archives. The trial holdings → (His boastful Argentina “Sassen interviews” are flagged in the research files as a partial, edited, disputed source — not a clean record.)

In the papers: the Detroit Jewish News, April 14, 1961, page 1 — the trial opens at Beth Ha-Am, Jerusalem. Scanned page →

Hermann Göring

Hitler's designated successor. Key document: his signed letter of July 31, 1941, charging Heydrich with “bringing about a total solution of the Jewish question in the German sphere of influence in Europe.” The document at Harvard →

In the papers: the Eugene Register-Guard, October 16, 1946, page 1, on his suicide the night before his hanging: “ACTOR TO THE LAST, vainglorious Hermann Goering… cheated the gibbet.” Scanned page →

Joseph Goebbels

Propaganda minister. Key documents: his diaries (the Fröhlich critical edition, from the glass microfiches found in Moscow) and the Sportpalast “total war” speech, February 18, 1943: “Do you want total war?” Read the speech excerpt (GHDI) →

In the papers: the Marlborough Express (New Zealand), May 7, 1945, printed Prague radio's claim that “Goebbels, Keitel and Bormann died fighting in the Reich Chancellery” — the regime's last fiction; he had killed himself on May 1. Scanned page →

Rudolf Höss

Commandant of Auschwitz. Key document: his Nuremberg affidavit (3868-PS), April 5, 1946 — read with the standing caution on his page: it is perpetrator testimony, and its headline figure (~3,000,000) is inflated against the transport-record reconstruction of about 1.1 million. Read the affidavit (Harvard) →

In the papers: the Detroit Jewish News, March 29, 1946, reported his first confession — with the inflated figures of those first reports, flagged as such in the research files. Scanned page →
