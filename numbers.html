The Numbers — Nazi Records & Population Counts

There is no single document that says “six million.” The total is built the way any total is built — by adding up separate, independent piles of evidence, created by different people in different places for different purposes, that agree with each other.

1. The killers' own running tallies

The Korherr Report — SS statistician Richard Korherr's report to Himmler, March 23, 1943: 1,274,166 Jews “transited through the camps in the General Government,” 1,873,549 “evacuations” overall, and Korherr's own balance sheet: between 1937 and the beginning of 1943, the number of Jews in Europe had declined by an estimated 4 million — close to half its population since 1933.

Read the Korherr Report (GHDI) →

The Höfle Telegram — an SS radio message of January 11, 1943, intercepted by the British (UK National Archives, HW 16/23): arrivals during 1942 at the four Operation Reinhard camps — Lublin/Majdanek 24,733; Belzec 434,508; Sobibor 101,370; Treblinka 713,555 — total 1,274,166. A completely separate document from Korherr's, matching it to the exact digit.

2. The killing units' own reports

The Jäger Report (December 1, 1941): one Einsatzkommando commander in Lithuania itemized 137,346 killings in five months, location by location, and concluded: “the goal of solving the Jewish problem for Lithuania has been achieved by EK 3.” Overall, the Einsatzgruppen and associated units killed about 1.3 million Jews in the East (Hilberg's figure).

3. Camp by camp, from transport records

Auschwitz: the Auschwitz-Birkenau Museum's reconstruction from transport records (Franciszek Piper): about 1.3 million deported, about 1.1 million murdered — about 960,000 of them Jews, most gassed on arrival and never registered. The “4 million” once on the Birkenau plaques came from a 1945 Soviet estimate based on crematoria capacity, not records; the plaques were replaced in 1990, and Western scholars never used the 4 million figure — so its removal subtracts nothing from the overall total.

4. The population counts

Europe's Jewish population: about 9.5 million in 1933 — about 3.5 million in 1950 (USHMM / American Jewish Year Book). Poland alone: 3,300,000 before the war; 45,000 remaining in Poland in 1945.

5. The historians' totals

  • Raul Hilberg (1961), from the German documents: ~5.1 million
  • The Nuremberg tribunal: 5.7 million
  • Lucy Dawidowicz (1975), demographic method: ~5.93 million
  • USHMM reconstruction: ~5.75–5.95 million
  • Yad Vashem holds 4.5–5 million victims identified by name

Different methods, the same range. And one caution the records themselves demand: the perpetrators wrote in euphemisms — “evacuation,” “special treatment,” “transited through” — and destroyed records in 1943–45. What survives is a floor, not a ceiling.

Sources — the original articles and documents

  • The Korherr Report, March 23, 1943 — the SS statistician's report to Himmler, document and translation at German History in Documents and Images (GHDI).
  • The Höfle Telegram, January 11, 1943 — the intercepted SS radio message: the document, its per-camp figures, and its archive reference (UK National Archives, HW 16/23), with the Witte–Tyas research that identified it.
  • The Jäger Report, December 1, 1941 — the full text of Einsatzkommando 3's itemized report, at JewishGen.
  • The Höttl affidavit (2738-PS), November 26, 1945 — Eichmann's estimate as reported by Wilhelm Höttl: one input among many, in the Harvard Nuremberg archive. (Plain-text version.)
  • Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum — “The research on the number of victims of the camp” — how Franciszek Piper rebuilt the Auschwitz toll from the transport records, and where the Soviet 4-million figure came from.
  • United States Holocaust Memorial Museum — “Documenting Numbers of Victims of the Holocaust and Nazi Persecution” — the population counts and category-by-category reconstruction.
  • Arolsen Archives — where the records live now: some 30 million documents on some 17.5 million people.
  • The Detroit Jewish News, April 14, 1961, page 1 — the Eichmann trial opens: how the numbers were put to a court (scanned page, Bentley Historical Library).
