Patton & the O'Donnell Column

New York Daily News, “Capitol Stuff,” October 3, 1945 — and the retraction, October 19, 1945

When General George S. Patton Jr. was removed from command of the U.S. 3rd Army in the autumn of 1945, the Daily News columnist John O'Donnell told his readers the removal showed:

“the secret and astoundingly effective might of this republic's foreign-born political leaders — such as Justice of the Supreme Court Felix Frankfurter, of Vienna, White House administrative assistant Dave (devious Dave) Niles alias Neyhus and the Latvian ex-rabbinical student now known as Sidney Hillman.”

O'Donnell's words as preserved by historian Matthew Pressman, who read the original column (Daily News, Oct. 3, 1945, p. 4) on Newspapers.com for his 2021 article in Modern American History: read the article →

O'Donnell also wrote that Secretary of the Treasury Morgenthau and Justice Frankfurter “bellowed in the White House” over Patton using the word “Jew” in reprimanding a soldier. The other papers answered him within days — the Detroit Jewish News quoted the column on October 12, 1945:

The Detroit Jewish News, Oct. 12, 1945, page 3 — the scanned page →

The retraction

Sixteen days after the column, on October 19, 1945, O'Donnell printed this, in his own words:

“On Oct. 3 this column attributed the removal of Gen. George S. Patton Jr. from his command of the U. S. 3rd Army... On the evidence, our statements in Capitol Stuff were untrue. We regret having made them.”

As quoted in The Jewish Post, Oct. 26, 1945, and The Sentinel, Oct. 25, 1945.

An honest note on the evidence: the full text of the October 3 column is not freely available online — the Daily News is not in Chronicling America. What survives openly is the column's own words as quoted, word for word, by the newspapers that answered it, and Pressman's reading of the original. Where the complete column can be read: Newspapers.com (paid), ProQuest Historical Newspapers (free from home with a New York Public Library card), or the Washington Times-Herald — which syndicated the column — on microfilm at the Library of Congress.
