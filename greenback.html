The Greenback Find

“THE NEW DEPARTURE” — The Anti-Monopolist, Saint Paul, Minnesota, April 4, 1878

One clipping in the collection showed a fragment of old print about greenbacks and gold. The question was simple: where did it actually come from? After a long hunt through books and pamphlets that turned out not to be the source, the passage was found — verified in the page's own text — in a Saint Paul newspaper of 1878, written by Stephen D. Dillaye.

The passage, exactly as printed

“The Greenback is the new departure. It is America for America. It is the American financial policy. It is the people for the people. It is the nation saying to all its enemies, ‘hands off, this is our money. Gold is good, but it is scarce, foreigners control it. Greenbacks are better. They are from the people for the people. They are the nation for the nation. They are Americans.’ To be free, a nation must control its own money. To be slaves to gold is to be slaves to the Rothchilds and German Jews. With Greenbacks for our currency and a money policy of our own free from all banking interference, (and above and beyond all foreign interference) which shall furnish an adequate supply for a perfect equality of right, such will give labor its reward and industry its recompense—this is the American policy.”

Spelling “Rothchilds” as printed, with no second s.

One page is the whole story here — but the hunt is documented too: the research files record every book and pamphlet checked and ruled out along the way (Coughlin's Social Justice, February 1940; the 2014 Sacred Truth pamphlet reprint; The New Ensign reprint of 2022; Peter Cooper, 1880; and ten Greenback-era books read in full), so the dead ends are on record alongside the find.
