What the Papers Said — Newspaper Coverage Outside Germany

What this file is: Beyond the German documents, this file sets out what newspapers and publications outside Germany said about Hitler, Himmler and the events in this folder, at the time or shortly after. Below are contemporary reports, quoted in the papers' own words, with the paper's name and date for each. Where the original page is paywalled, that is flagged plainly with the exact citation instead of guessed text.

The honest headline finding: none of this was secret or invented after the fact. American, British and Jewish papers reported the anti-Jewish laws in 1935, the camps as they were liberated in spring 1945, the documents at Nuremberg in 1945–46, and the Eichmann trial in 1961 — in real time, in print.

1. The Nuremberg Laws — American papers, November 1935

When the November 14, 1935 decree defined who counted as a Jew under the Nuremberg Laws, United Press wired it to American papers the next day under the headline "Germany Denies Jews Citizenship":

"BERLIN, Nov. 15 (U.P.) — The status of the German Jew under the Nazi Reich was fixed finally today, after months of study, by a decree in the official gazette. Chief provisions are: 1—Jews are not citizens, but only state subjects, and only citizens may vote. 2—All Jewish civil servants must resign by December 31… A Jew under the meaning of the law is one who has one or more Jewish [grandparents]."

  • Collected by the US Holocaust Memorial Museum's History Unfolded project (927 American articles on this event are in that database): https://newspapers.ushmm.org/historical-article/1935-germany-denies-jews-citizenship-21277
  • The original page image sits behind Newspapers.com (paywall) — flagged, not guessed.
  • The New York Times of September 16, 1935 led with the flag story — headline: "REICH ADOPTS SWASTIKA AS NATION'S OFFICIAL FLAG" — with the anti-Jewish laws in the sub-headline: "Anti-Jewish Laws Passed. Non-'Aryans' Deprived of Citizenship and Right to Intermarry" (headlines as quoted in Whitman, Hitler's American Model, 2017; the Times' own archive is paywalled).

2. Belsen — the Manchester Guardian, April 14, 1945 (British)

The Manchester Guardian's war correspondent David Woodward reported the negotiated handover of Bergen-Belsen the day before British troops entered it:

"As a result of negotiations carried out between British and German officers during a local truce on this sector British troops will take over from the SS and the Wehrmacht the task of guarding a vast concentration camp at Belsen, a few miles north-west of Celle, which contains approximately 60,000 prisoners, both criminals and anti-Nazis."

  • Manchester Guardian, April 14, 1945 (Guardian archive reprint). Two days later (April 16, 1945) the Guardian's report on what the Americans found at Buchenwald carried freed prisoners' testimony that "the outstanding place of extermination in the world was Auschwitz near Cracow" — British readers were reading the name Auschwitz, and what it was, in April 1945.

3. Dachau liberated — Greencastle Daily Banner (Indiana), April 30, 1945

Small-town America read the Associated Press wire the day after Dachau was liberated. The Greencastle Daily Banner (Greencastle, Indiana), front page, April 30, 1945:

"The notorious Dachau concentration camp seven miles north of Munich — the first and blackest of the political death camps established in the early days of the Hitler regime — was over-run by the Seventh army yesterday. There the Yanks killed or captured 300 SS guards and liberated 32,000 political and religious prisoners who greeted their rescuers with hysterical joy. For hundreds and perhaps thousands of Dachau's other inmates the Americans came too late. Fifty boxcars were found on a nearby railroad siding, loaded with bodies, torture chambers, gas boxes, and other paraphernalia of terror that the Nazi guards were attempting to remove."

  • Scanned page: Hoosier State Chronicles (Indiana's newspaper archive, feeding Chronicling America), Greencastle Daily Banner, April 30, 1945, p. 1 — reproduced and cited at https://blog.history.in.gov/tag/muncie-post-democrat/
  • Same day, the Chicago Daily Times headlined it: "FREE 32,000 IN HORROR CAMP."

4. Dachau — New York Herald Tribune, May 1, 1945

Marguerite Higgins was among the first correspondents inside Dachau. Her dispatch opened:

"Troops of the United States 7th Army liberated 33,000 prisoners this afternoon at this first and largest of the Nazi concentration camps. Some of the prisoners had endured for eleven years the horrors of notorious Dachau… The barracks at Dachau, like those at Buchenwald, had the stench of death and sickness."

  • New York Herald Tribune, May 1, 1945. Full text reprinted at https://deadlineartists.com/contributor-samples/the-liberation-of-dachau-marguerite-higgins-new-york-herald-tribune/ — the Tribune's own archive is paywalled (ProQuest); flagged, not guessed.

5. Nuremberg — the Höttl affidavit in the American press, December 1945

In December 1945 American papers (e.g., the Asbury Park Press, New Jersey, collected in USHMM's History Unfolded) reported the affidavit of SS officer Wilhelm Höttl, who swore that Eichmann had told him in August 1944:

"Approximately four million Jews had been killed in the various extermination camps while an additional two million met death in other ways, the major part of which were shot by operational squads of the Security Police during the campaign against Russia."

  • The affidavit itself (Nuremberg document PS-2738, November 26, 1945), readable in full at Harvard Law School's Nuremberg Trials Project: https://nuremberg.law.harvard.edu/documents/1195-affidavit-concerning-a-conversation?mode=text
  • Flagged plainly, for honesty about sources: Höttl's affidavit is one perpetrator's sworn report of a conversation — testimony, not a count. It is not what the modern totals rest on. The totals in this folder rest on the regime's own statistics and transport records (Korherr, Höfle, the camp-by-camp reconstructions) and on the population counts. Eichmann himself later disputed Höttl's account. The affidavit matters here because it shows the six-million scale being stated on the record, in court and in the papers, in 1945 — by an SS officer, from Eichmann's own office's figures.

6. The Eichmann trial — the Jewish press, April 1961

The Detroit Jewish News — a paper already in this research — put the trial on its front page. Its JTA correspondent Eliahu Salpeter reported from Jerusalem (April 14, 1961):

"…the right of the State of Israel to bring the ex-Gestapo colonel to justice for directing the mass-murder of 6,000,000 Jews."

A week earlier its editorial page (April 7, 1961, "Eichmann Trial — An End to Genocide") wrote:

"Eichmann will have defense counsel at his trial for having master-minded the murder of six million Jews. Even the worst criminals are entitled to legal aid, and Israel has assured it for him."

  • The actual scanned front page (the paper as it looked): https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/djnews/djn.1961.04.14.001/1
  • The scanned editorial page: https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/djnews/djn.1961.04.07.001/4
  • Small-town America got it by AP wire too — the East Liverpool Review (Ohio), April 17, 1961, quoted prosecutor Gideon Hausner's opening: "Here with me stand six million prosecutors. But alas, they cannot rise and level a finger at the man in the prisoner's dock. Their blood cries to heaven but their voices cannot be heard." Full issue text: https://archive.org/stream/east-liverpool-review-1961-04-17/east-liverpool-review-1961-04-17_djvu.txt
