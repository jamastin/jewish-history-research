King Joseph and his father Aaron II — the last kings

Gathered for James, 2026-11-19, during the review of the Khazar research. Sources: Joseph's own Reply to Hasdai ibn Shaprut (c. 954–961), the Schechter Letter (Cairo Geniza, published 1912), the Russian Primary Chronicle, Ibn Hawqal, and the standard ruler lists (Dunlop 1954; Golb & Pritsak 1982; Zhirohov & Nicolle). Where the record is silent, this file says so.

Was Joseph Jewish? The name question

Yes — by religion, upbringing, and kingship. But Joseph himself draws a line his readers often miss:

  • The heading of his own letter calls him "Joseph the King, son of Aaron the King, the Turk." His own document labels him a Turk by people, a Jew by faith.
  • In the letter he traces his blood to Togarmah, son of Japheth — a Turkic ancestor, not a son of Israel. His faith he traces to Abraham. He never claims Israelite descent.
  • His Hebrew name (Yosef ben Aharon) was his birth name in a converted royal house. After Obadiah's reform (synagogues, yeshivot, Bible/Mishnah/Talmud taught), the dynasty gave its sons Torah names for generation after generation. Joseph's own list of his line: Bulan → Obadiah → Hezekiah → Manasseh → Hanukkah → Isaac → Zebulun → Moses (Manasseh II) → Nisi → Aaron I → Menahem → Benjamin → Aaron II → Joseph. Every name in the later line is biblical Hebrew. If the conversion was c. 740, Joseph was born roughly 200 years — eight to ten generations — into his family's Judaism.

Joseph (reigned c. 940–965)

  • Son of Aaron II; took the throne about 940.
  • Married an Alan princess — a marriage his father arranged (see Aaron II below).
  • Almost everything known about him comes from his own Reply to Hasdai ibn Shaprut of Córdoba (c. 954–961): his genealogy, the conversion account, the tribute list of subject peoples, and his description of guarding the Volga mouth against the Rus'.
  • His own words on that duty: "I dwell at the delta of the Itil and, by God's help, I guard the mouth of the river and do not permit the Russians who come in ships to pass… I war with them. If I left them in peace for one hour, they would destroy the entire land of the Ishmaelites up to Baghdad."
  • His own words on his reign, written c. 960: "Since that day, when my fathers entered into this religion, the God of Israel has humbled all of their enemies, subjecting every folk and tongue round about them, whether Christian, Muslim, or pagan. No one has been able to stand before them to this day. All of them are tributary." He signs himself "the son of a King, and the descendant of kings."
  • Within five to nine years of that letter, Sviatoslav of Kiev broke the kingdom: Sarkel taken 965 (Primary Chronicle), Itil sacked 968/969 (Ibn Hawqal: "no grape or raisin remained").
  • His fate is not recorded. No source names the Khazar king who faced Sviatoslav in 965 (the Chronicle says only "their Prince, the Kagan"), and no source records Joseph's death, capture, or burial. Joseph is the last Khazar king known by name from the empire's life; he may have still been on the throne at the fall or have died just before it — the record does not say.
  • Open scholarly question: whether Joseph and his Bulanid line were khagans or beks (the executive "kings" under a sacred khagan). Joseph never mentions a superior, so the two roles may have merged by the 900s.
  • Muslim sources say the Khazar ruler after the disaster accepted Islam and Khwarazmian protection in exchange for help — a rump state — but they do not name him, so it cannot honestly be identified as Joseph.

Aaron II, his father (reigned c. 920s–939/940)

  • Aaron ben Benjamin, son of King Benjamin, father of Joseph.
  • His one recorded deed comes from the Schechter Letter: Byzantium incited the Alans — Khazar allies under Aaron's father Benjamin — to attack Khazaria. Aaron hired Oghuz Turks as mercenaries, defeated the Alans, and captured their king alive.
  • Rather than execute his prisoner, Aaron treated him with respect, took an oath of fealty, and sealed the peace by marrying the Alan king's daughter to his son Joseph.
  • Pritsak dated this war to the early 920s (early in the reign of Emperor Romanos I — the same Romanos who later bribed the Rus' king HLGW to attack Khazaria, the Pesakh episode in the same letter).
  • Aaron died about 939/940 and was succeeded by Joseph. Nothing else about him survives: no letter, no second deed, no recorded words.

After them

  • David (c. 986–988): a Khazar ruler near Taman, known only from the later ruler lists.
  • George Tzula (d. 1016): the last man called khagan, ruling at Kerch in Crimea; captured in 1016 by a joint Rus'–Byzantine expedition (Kedrenos/Skylitzes; Dunlop pp. 251–252), his state destroyed. His Christian name suggests he was likely a Christian ruler of Khazar stock, half a century after Itil fell — not a Jewish king.
