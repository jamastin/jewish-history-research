Hermann Göring (1893–1946)

The defendants in the dock at the Nuremberg trials, 1945. Source: Wikimedia Commons

Who he was: The regime's number two — Reich Marshal, commander of the Luftwaffe, head of the Four-Year Plan (and so, for years, the man in charge of the regime's economic war on the Jews, including the billion-mark “atonement fine” after Kristallnacht), and Hitler's designated successor. He was the senior defendant at Nuremberg, was convicted on all counts, and killed himself with a cyanide capsule on 15 October 1946, the night before his scheduled hanging.

Key documents

1. The “total solution” authorization — 31 July 1941

Göring's signed letter to Reinhard Heydrich (Nuremberg document 710-PS, exhibit USA-509) is the written commission behind the Wannsee Conference. Its key line:

“I hereby charge you with making all necessary preparations, with regard to organisational and financial matters, for bringing about a total solution of the Jewish question in the German sphere of influence in Europe.”

The letter also ordered Heydrich to send him an overall plan “before long.” At Nuremberg, Göring admitted the signature was his but argued over the translation — insisting the word was “complementing,” not “completing,” the 1939 emigration task; the exchange is in the trial transcript for 20 March 1946. - The document at Harvard's Nuremberg Trials Project: Nuremberg Trials Project (Harvard Law School) - Göring cross-examined on it, 20 March 1946 (Avalon Project transcript): avalon.law.yale.edu

2. His Nuremberg testimony — March 1946

Göring was the first defendant to testify (13–22 March 1946) and used the stand to defend the regime; his cross-examination by Justice Robert H. Jackson is one of the trial's set pieces. The full proceedings are in the Avalon Project's Nuremberg volumes (his testimony runs across volumes 9–10).

Researched 2026-11-21. Translation note: English renderings of the 31 July 1941 letter vary slightly (“total” / “complete” / “overall solution”); the German word is Gesamtlösung.

In the papers

Newspaper coverage of Göring, verified 2026-11-21.

  • The New York Times, 14 March 1946 — Göring on the witness stand at Nuremberg, under the heading “Goering Defends Nazi Suppression From Witness Stand in Nuremberg,” with coverage continuing on page 7 and a trial photograph. ⚠ Verified from a dealer's listing of the original issue (RareNewspapers.com); the Times archive itself is paywalled, so no text beyond the heading is quoted.
  • The Detroit Jewish News, 29 March 1946, page 3 — the Jewish press's wire summary of his testimony: “Ex-Reichsmarshal Herman Goering, testifying in his defense at the Nuremberg War Crime Trial, told the International Military Tribunal that both Joseph Goebbels and Heinrich Himmler were more rabid Jew-baiters than he. He said defendant Julius Streicher was violently anti-Jewish, but dismissed him as not a member of the Government.” Scanned page (Bentley Library): Detroit Jewish News archive (University of Michigan)
  • Eugene Register-Guard (Eugene, Oregon), 16 October 1946, page 1 — his suicide, hours before the hangings: “Hermann Goering, who ended his life mysteriously in the agony of poison… died in an agonized suicide less than two hours before he was to have met his fate on the gallows.” The paper's verdict line: “ACTOR TO THE LAST, vainglorious Hermann Goering, No. 1 member of the Hitler gang, cheated the gibbet in the closing minutes of the last act at Nuernberg Tuesday night.” Scanned page (University of Oregon newspaper archive): Historic Oregon Newspapers
