John O'Donnell — his own subfolder

Created 2026-11-20 at James's request: "make a O'Donnell subfolder and look up his other work."

Who he was: John O'Donnell, Washington columnist for the New York Daily News; his column was called "Capitol Stuff."

Why he has a folder here: his October 3, 1945 "Capitol Stuff" column claimed Gen. George S. Patton Jr. was removed from command of the U.S. 3rd Army because of Jewish political leaders in Washington (Felix Frankfurter, David Niles "alias Neyhus," Sidney Hillman). Jewish newspapers and others answered it across October–November 1945, and on October 19, 1945 O'Donnell printed a retraction: "On the evidence, our statements in Capitol Stuff were untrue. We regret having made them."

Already saved (in the General Patton subfolder, where the Patton walk-through lives): - general-patton/odonnell-original-column-1945-10-03-quoted-words.md — the column's surviving exact words, with footnotes (added 2026-11-20 at James's request): John O'Donnell as original author; Matthew Pressman as the historian who read the original column (Daily News, Oct. 3, 1945, p. 4, via Newspapers.com) for his 2021 article in Modern American History. - general-patton/expanded-search-odonnell-outside-loc.md — the expanded search for the full column outside the Library of Congress. - The papers that answered him: Der Tog (Oct 7 & 16, 1945), Commentator (Oct 25, 1945), Sentinel (Oct 25, 1945), Union Times (Nov 10, 1945), Evening Star (Oct 31, 1945).

Status of the full column: not freely available online as of 2026-11-20; a deep search for a free copy is running (James asked 2026-11-20). Where it is available: Newspapers.com (paywall, Daily News, Oct 3, 1945, p. 4); ProQuest Historical Newspapers via library access; Washington Times-Herald (which syndicated the column) on microfilm at the Library of Congress.

This folder: for O'Donnell's other work — his biography and his other columns and controversies beyond the Patton column. Research started 2026-11-20.

Files in this folder: - john-odonnell-biography.md — his life: John Parsons O'Donnell (born July 23, 1896, Somerville, Mass.; died Dec. 17, 1961, Washington, D.C.); Hearst's New York American 1923–27; Daily News from 1927; Washington bureau chief and "Capitol Stuff" from 1933 (started jointly with his wife Doris Fleeson; solo after their 1942 divorce); retired 1961. Includes his reputation in his contemporaries' words, his marriages, a picture section (a verified 1938 photo of O'Donnell and Fleeson is saved in images/ and shown at the top of the file; a licensed Getty photo of him interviewing Legs Diamond also exists), and cautions about errors in Wikipedia's article on him. - odonnell-other-work.md — his other work and controversies beyond the Patton column, with exact quotes: the April 17, 1941 Navy-escort story FDR denounced as "a deliberate lie"; the Philadelphia Record's "Naziphile" editorial and his libel suit (won, $8,000; Pa. Supreme Court 1947), including the sworn testimony of five fellow correspondents about his 1940 talk on Hitler and the Jews and his own admissions on the stand; FDR's Iron Cross insult (Dec. 18, 1942); the June 1943 WAAC column and its forced retraction; new named details of the 1945 advertiser boycott; the March 27, 1950 State Department column of Lavender Scare fame and the Aug. 14, 1953 anti-foreign-aid column (both via Pressman 2021, attributed); and smaller surviving columns (1944 "over here," 1951 chess/poker, 1955 and 1959 clippings). No book by him was found. - images/odonnell-and-fleeson-1938.jpg — John O'Donnell and Doris Fleeson on President Roosevelt's special train, 1938 (from WednesdaysWomen's Fleeson profile; downloaded and visually verified 2026-11-20).
