The Population Counts — the Demographic Check

The method: count the Jews of Europe before, count them after, and account for the difference. Births, deaths from natural causes, and emigration are all tracked in the same records — so a gap of millions cannot hide inside them. This check does not depend on any Nazi document at all; it uses censuses and Jewish community statistics, including the American Jewish Yearbook figures the US Holocaust Memorial Museum uses.

The headline

Jews in Europe
1933 ~9.5 million (about 60% of the world's ~15.3 million Jews)
1950 ~3.5 million

A drop of about six million. Emigration explains only a small part: the countries Jews fled to (the US, Britain, Palestine, the Soviet interior) recorded their arrivals, and those numbers are in the hundreds of thousands, not millions. The missing millions appear in no country's postwar population — because they were dead. Two out of every three Jews in Europe were killed.

Country by country (largest losses)

Figures are the USHMM's 1933 population table against postwar counts. Borders moved and sources count differently (in-country survivors vs. all survivors including refugees), so ranges are given where the sources genuinely differ — that is the honest state of the numbers.

Country Jews, 1933 Jews remaining after the war Note
Poland 3,300,000 ~45,000 in Poland in 1945; ~80,000–300,000 counting survivors who had fled or been deported into the USSR and returned by 1946 Over 90% murdered — the largest single loss
Soviet Union 3,020,000 ~2,100,000 Most Soviet Jews lived outside the occupied zone or were evacuated east; in the occupied territories, murder was near-total
Romania 756,000 ~280,000–360,000 Romania's own regime murdered ~280,000–380,000 Jews in Bessarabia, Bukovina and Transnistria
Hungary 445,000 (1933 borders); ~825,000 in the enlarged wartime borders ~190,000–200,000 Most Hungarian Jews were deported to Auschwitz in spring–summer 1944
Germany 525,000 ~37,000 Roughly half of German Jews emigrated before the war; of those who remained in 1939, most were murdered
Netherlands 140,000 ~30,000 About 102,000 of 140,000 Dutch Jews murdered — the highest proportion in Western Europe
Greece 73,000 ~10,000 The ancient community of Salonika (~50,000) was deported to Auschwitz in 1943 and effectively wiped out
Lithuania ~155,000–168,000 ~20,000 Compare the Jäger Report (einsatzgruppen-reports.md): one SS unit itemized 137,346 murders in Lithuania in five months of 1941

Even the SS's own statistician saw it

This demographic picture is not a postwar invention imposed on the record — it is in the record. In March 1943, the SS's Inspector for Statistics, Richard Korherr, reported to Himmler that the number of Jews in Europe had "declined by an estimated 4 million" between 1937 and the beginning of 1943, and that European Jewry had "lost close to half of its population" since 1933 (see korherr-report.md). The killing still had almost two years to run.

Where the names are

Population arithmetic is checked at the level of individual people by Yad Vashem's Central Database of Shoah Victims' Names, which has so far recovered the names and fates of more than 4.5 million individual victims — approaching five million — from Pages of Testimony, deportation lists, camp records and archives. The count keeps growing as records are digitized (see the-full-picture.md on the Arolsen Archives).
