Reinhard Heydrich (1904–1942)

Who he was: Himmler's chief lieutenant. He built the SD (the SS intelligence service), took over the Gestapo and criminal police, and from September 1939 headed the Reich Security Main Office (RSHA) — the office that combined the Gestapo, Kripo and SD. The Einsatzgruppen, the mobile killing squads that followed the army into Poland and the Soviet Union, operated under his RSHA command structure. From September 1941 he was also acting Reich Protector of Bohemia and Moravia. Czech and Slovak agents of Operation Anthropoid mortally wounded him in Prague on 27 May 1942; he died on 4 June 1942.

Key documents

1. The Wannsee Protocol — 20 January 1942

Heydrich chaired the Wannsee Conference, the 90-minute meeting of senior officials at Am Großen Wannsee 56–58, Berlin, called to line up the government ministries behind the “Final Solution.” The surviving minutes — recorded by Adolf Eichmann — list some 11 million Jews across Europe, country by country, and describe deportation “to the East” in labour columns, with the “final remnant” to be “treated accordingly.” Only one copy of the protocol (copy 16 of 30) survived the war; it was found in Foreign Office files in 1947. - Read the full protocol (GHDI): https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/the-wannsee-protocol-january-20-1942

2. Göring's authorization to Heydrich — 31 July 1941

The written commission behind Wannsee: Reich Marshal Hermann Göring's letter charging Heydrich with preparing a “total solution of the Jewish question” (Nuremberg document 710-PS). The full text and quote are filed in Göring's own subfolder. - The document at Harvard's Nuremberg Trials Project: https://nuremberg.law.harvard.edu/documents/451565-instructions-to-heydrich-to-prepare?mode=image

3. The Einsatzgruppen under his office

The four Einsatzgruppen (A–D) were organised under Heydrich's security-police apparatus and reported back through the RSHA; their own situation reports — tallying mass shootings day by day — became core Nuremberg evidence. Heydrich's 21 September 1939 express letter to the Einsatzgruppen chiefs set out policy in occupied Poland, including concentrating Jews in cities near rail lines. - Background and biography (Britannica): https://www.britannica.com/biography/Reinhard-Heydrich

Researched 2026-11-21.

In the papers

Newspaper coverage of Heydrich, verified 2026-11-21.

  • An American newspaper (Associated Press), 4 June 1942, page 1 — his death announced from Berlin broadcasts: “Reinhard Heydrich, thirty-eight, reichsprotector for Bohemia and Moravia, died in Prague this morning of wounds suffered in an attempt on his life May 27, it was announced officially today.” The paper called him “der henker” — “the hangman” — and reported reprisals already under way. The clipping is preserved in the US Holocaust Memorial Museum's History Unfolded database (the record does not name the specific paper; the clipping was researched on Newspapers.com, which is paywalled): https://newspapers.ushmm.org/historical-article/1942-nazi-hangman-dies-in-prague-of-his-wounds-66352
  • The Times (London), 11 June 1942 — a British paper reports Lidice under the headline “A CZECH VILLAGE WIPED OUT — BARBAROUS VENGEANCE FOR HEYDRICH”: “all the men of the village of Lidice, near Kladno… have been shot on suspicion of harbouring the men who shot Heydrich,” the women deported to a concentration camp and the children sent to “educational centres.” (The cutting gives Lidice's population as about 12,000 — the paper's own figure, and wrong; Berlin radio itself said 483.) Scanned cutting (IBCC Digital Archive, University of Lincoln): https://ibccdigitalarchive.lincoln.ac.uk/items/browse?collection=1339&page=6&sort_field=Dublin+Core%2CTitle&sort_dir=d&output=omeka-xml
  • An American newspaper (Associated Press), 11 June 1942 — headline “CZECH DEATH TOLL FROM VENGEANCE REPORTED AT 306,” reporting that the purge “wiped the little town of Lidice off the map” and that all Lidice's men had been shot “on the charge that the population sheltered the assassins of Heydrich, Nazi ruler of Bohemia and Moravia.” Preserved in USHMM History Unfolded (specific paper not named on the record; researched on Newspapers.com): https://newspapers.ushmm.org/historical-article/1942-czech-death-toll-from-nazi-vengeance-reported-at-306-54094
