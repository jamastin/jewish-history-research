The four colonies in the 1894 report — identified

The Bill Barlow's Budget article (May 15, 1895) summarizing the Jewish Colonization Association's report for 1894 says "four colonies were established" (200,000 acres, 563 families) but does not name them. Identification below is reconstructed from the Jewish Encyclopedia (1901–1906), "Agricultural Colonies in the Argentine Republic," and JewishGen colony records — it is a strong reconstruction, not a quote from the report itself.

The four

  1. Moïseville (Moisés Ville), Santa Fe province — the oldest. Founded 1890 by the Russian immigrants who arrived on the Weser in August 1889; reorganized by the JCA in 1891. Nearly 60,000 acres including the Virginia and Santa Elena estates. (JE: "the oldest of the Jewish Agricultural Colonies in Argentina.")
  2. Mauricio, Buenos Aires province — one of the first two tracts set apart by the JCA in 1891, named for the Baron himself. About 62,000 acres (25,000 hectares).
  3. Lucienville, Entre Ríos (at Basavilbaso) — founded October 1893 (first groups Novabuco and Aquerman), named for the Baron's son Lucien, who died in 1887. In place well before the end of 1894. (JewishGen KehilaLinks, Basavilbaso settlement page; Posen Library Lucienville report.)
  4. Clara, Entre Ríos — established by the JCA in 1894, named for Baroness Clara. The JE says its main body of colonists "was organized in Russia in 1894; ten groups, of about forty families each, being formed" — which matches the 1895 article's own words exactly: "Ten groups, comprising an average of about forty families, about 400 families in all, were settled in the Argentine in the year 1894."

Confidence note

  • Moïseville, Mauricio, and Clara are certain — they are the colonies the JE describes as existing in this period, and Clara's "ten groups" match the article's 1894 settlement figures one-for-one.
  • Lucienville is the strong fourth: founded October 1893, so it existed before 1895, and no other JCA colony in Argentina is documented as established by the end of 1894. Acreage is consistent (Moïseville ~60,000 + Mauricio ~62,000 leaves ~78,000 for Clara and Lucienville together, within the article's 200,000 total), though the exact per-colony split in the 1894 report itself was not recovered.
  • Sources: Jewish Encyclopedia, "Agricultural Colonies in the Argentine Republic" (jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/905); JewishGen KehilaLinks Basavilbaso settlement page; Posen Library, "Report from Lucienville Agricultural Colony."
