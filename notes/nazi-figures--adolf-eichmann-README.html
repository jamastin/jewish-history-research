Adolf Eichmann (1906–1962)

Who he was: SS-Obersturmbannführer in charge of Section IV B 4 of the Reich Security Main Office — the desk that organised the deportation of Jews from across Europe to the ghettos and killing centres: timetables, trains, and the machinery of removal. He took the minutes at Wannsee. After the war he escaped to Argentina; Israeli agents captured him in Buenos Aires in May 1960. He was tried in Jerusalem in 1961, convicted, and hanged on 1 June 1962 — the only judicial execution in Israel's history.

Key documents

1. The Jerusalem trial record — 1961

The trial before the District Court of Jerusalem (April–December 1961) is one of the most fully documented proceedings of the century: 114 sessions, survivor testimony, and thousands of documents. The Israel State Archives holds the court files of both the District Court and the Supreme Court appeal, the Bureau 06 police files, and the trial films and audio (record groups RG30, RG31, RG79, RG145, RG146). English transcripts were published in book form (The Trial of Adolf Eichmann, Ministry of Justice, 1994) and are online through the Nizkor Project. - Israel State Archives — the Eichmann trial holdings: https://catalog.archives.gov.il/site/en/chapter/material-trial-isa/ - Trial film in the US Holocaust Memorial Museum collection: https://collections.ushmm.org/search/catalog/irn1001039

2. The pre-trial interrogation — Bureau 06, 1960–61

Before the trial, Eichmann was interrogated for months by Captain Avner Less of the Israeli police's Bureau 06 — the special unit built to prepare the case. The interrogation ran from 29 May 1960 into January 1961; its transcripts (thousands of pages) are the primary record of Eichmann explaining his own role, and they were tested against documents in court. The interrogation files and recordings sit in the Israel State Archives' police record group (RG79).

3. The Sassen interviews — ⚠ handle with care

In 1956–57, in Argentina, the Dutch journalist and former SS man Willem Sassen recorded long interviews in which Eichmann, among fellow exiles, spoke far more boastfully about his role than he later did in Jerusalem. Reliability flag: the Sassen material is a partial, edited transcript of tapes — Eichmann disputed parts of it at trial, only portions of the recordings and his handwritten corrections survive, and historians use it cautiously and alongside the documents, never as a clean verbatim record. Its courtroom handling is itself in the trial transcript (see Session 72, where the handwriting evidence on the transcript pages is examined). - Trial transcript, Session 72 (Nizkor): https://www.nizkor.org/session-072-07-eichmann-adolf/

In the papers

Newspaper coverage of Eichmann, verified 2026-11-21. The Jewish press covered him more heavily than anyone — the Jewish Telegraphic Agency (JTA) alone published more than 600 Eichmann items between the capture and the execution.

  • Jewish Telegraphic Agency, 23 May 1960 — JTA reported Prime Minister David Ben-Gurion's surprise announcement to the Knesset that Eichmann was in Israeli hands: “For a moment, there was silence in the chamber. Then there was a burst of wild applause.” (The capture itself, in Argentina on 11 May, was not yet described.) Retold from JTA's own archive coverage by the St. Louis Jewish Light: https://stljewishlight.org/world-news/heres-how-jta-covered-the-real-life-story-of-operation-finale-and-eichmanns-capture/
  • The Detroit Jewish News, 3 June 1960, page 7 — “Report Eichmann Captured in Argentina” (continued from page 1), quoting Buenos Aires' La Razón: “Adolf Eichmann, the Nazi who directed the killing of 6,000,000 European Jews, was captured by Israel agents in Argentina.” Scanned page (Bentley Library): https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/djnews/djn.1960.06.03.001/7
  • The Detroit Jewish News, 7 April 1961, page 4 — editorial on the eve of the trial: “All eyes are directed at Jerusalem where, starting next Tuesday, proceedings will commence in the most important court case in mankind's history.” Scanned page: https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/djnews/djn.1961.04.07.001/4
  • The Detroit Jewish News, 14 April 1961, page 1 — the trial opens, reported by JTA's Eliahu Salpeter: “With the eyes of the world directed toward the courtroom at Beth Ha-Am as the trial opened Tuesday, Adolf Eichmann's attorney used every legal stratagem to contest the jurisdiction and competence of the tribunal…” The paper's editor, Philip Slomovitz, reported from Jerusalem himself. Scanned page: https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/djnews/djn.1961.04.14.001/1
