Auschwitz — How the Number Was Rebuilt, and the "4 Million" Story

The current figure, and how it was arrived at

The Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum's historian Franciszek Piper rebuilt the count in the 1980s–90s from the transport records — train schedules, deportation lists from every country, and camp records — counting who was sent in, and subtracting those registered, transferred out, released, or known to have survived. His reconstruction, now the museum's official figure:

Group Deported to Auschwitz Murdered
Jews 1,095,000 960,000
Poles (non-Jewish) 147,000 74,000
Roma 23,000 21,000
Soviet prisoners of war 15,000 15,000
Others 25,000 ~12,000–15,000
Total at least 1.3 million at least 1.1 million

Of the 960,000 Jewish victims, about 865,000 were gassed on arrival and never registered in the camp at all. That single fact explains most misunderstandings about Auschwitz records.

The museum on its own research (podcast transcript, Dr. Piotr Setkiewicz, head of the museum's Research Center): https://www.auschwitz.org/en/education/e-learning/podcast/the-research-on-the-number-of-victims-of-the-camp/

The death books — what they do and don't cover

The camp kept death registers (Sterbebücher) for registered prisoners only. Forty-six volumes survive, recording almost 69,000 deaths between July 29, 1941 and December 31, 1943 — names, dates, prisoner numbers. The Auschwitz Museum states the limitation itself: the overwhelming majority of victims — the Jews sent straight from the ramp to the gas chambers — were never registered, and their deaths appear in no camp register. The death books are real evidence of camp mortality; they were never a count of the murdered, and no historian uses them as one. The totals come from the transport side: how many people the trains demonstrably brought in.

(The books themselves have a paper trail: the SS took them east as the camp was evacuated; the Soviets seized them; after the fall of communism they were handed over via the Red Cross and are now at the museum and searchable online.)

The "4 million" figure — where it actually came from

This is the claim the Facebook video leans on, so here is the whole story, from the museum's own account:

  1. May 1945. The Soviet Extraordinary State Commission investigated the liberated camp. Unable to count victims from records, its engineers calculated the maximum theoretical capacity of the Birkenau crematoria — how many bodies the ovens could physically burn if run continuously — multiplied by the days of operation. That produced ~5.2 million; allowing for interruptions, the commission settled on "about four million" as, in its words, the most credible figure. It was a capacity calculation, not a count of people. It was published in Pravda on May 7, 1945, and used by the Soviet prosecution at Nuremberg.
  2. 1947–1990. The figure became the official number of the Polish communist state and was carved on the memorial plaques at Birkenau (the international monument was unveiled in 1967). Under communism, the museum's historians could not publish against it.
  3. Western historians never used it. Gerald Reitlinger's The Final Solution (1953) put Auschwitz at roughly 800,000–900,000. Raul Hilberg's The Destruction of the European Jews (1961) put it at about one million. The Western scholarly range was 1–1.5 million all along — which is why the plaque change did not move the overall total: the Soviet four million was never one of its building blocks.
  4. 1990. After communism fell, the museum formally adopted Piper's transport-based research (~1.1 million) and the plaques were replaced; the new plaque speaks of "a million and a half" in some wordings and the museum's research figure is 1.1 million victims of 1.3 million deported.

The honest bottom line: an early Soviet estimate, produced by a crude method in the chaos of 1945 and frozen in stone by a communist government, was corrected by the museum's own historians as soon as they were free to publish — using the deportation records. The correction is a case of the evidence winning. It does not subtract anything from the six-million total, because that total was never built on the four-million figure.
