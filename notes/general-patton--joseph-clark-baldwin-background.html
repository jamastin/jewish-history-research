Background check — Congressman Joseph Clark Baldwin

Requested by James on 2026-11-20, after the Der Tog piece of October 7, 1945 (article #5 in this folder), where Baldwin called John O'Donnell's charge that American Jews were "out to get" Gen. Patton "just silly nonsense" and "ridiculously ridiculous."

Who he was

  • Joseph Clark Baldwin (born January 11, 1897, New York City — died October 27, 1957, New York City), Republican U.S. Representative from New York's 17th district.
  • Educated at St. Paul's School, Concord, N.H. (1916) and Harvard University (1920).
  • World War I: enlisted in the Navy in 1917, transferred to the Army in 1918; served overseas as a private in the Machine Gun Company of the 305th Infantry, then received a commission and commanded the First Platoon, Machine Gun Company, 39th Infantry; officer of the French Legion of Honor.
  • Journalism first: political reporter for the New York Tribune; associate editor of the North Westchester Times, 1922–1930; founded his own public relations firm in 1930.
  • New York politics: Board of Aldermen 1929–1934; New York State Senate 1935–1936; delegate to the New York State constitutional convention, 1938; New York City Council 1937–1941.
  • Congress: elected as a Republican to the 77th Congress to fill the vacancy left by the death of Kenneth F. Simpson; re-elected to the 78th and 79th Congresses; served March 11, 1941 – January 3, 1947. Lost his bid for renomination in 1946.
  • After Congress: representative for United Dye and Chemical Corp. and William Recht Co., Inc. Wrote a book, Flowers for the Judge (Coward-McCann, 1950). A 73-page oral history he recorded in 1950 is held by Columbia University's Oral History Archives.
  • Died in the Veterans Administration Hospital in Manhattan, October 27, 1957; buried in Woodlawn Cemetery, the Bronx.

Family lineage

  • Born Joseph Clark Baldwin III. His father was Joseph Clark Baldwin Jr. (born April 23, 1871, New York City — died December 29, 1937) and his mother Fanny Taylor Baldwin (born June 4, 1874 — died January 8, 1948). His parents married April 15, 1896, at Mamaroneck, N.Y. He dropped the "III" after his father's death in 1937.
  • His grandfather was Joseph Clark Baldwin (born 1838) and his grandmother Emma Jane Mood (born 1840) — per the Day Family Tree / Penfield Family Genealogy.
  • The Political Graveyard traces him as a sixth great-grandson of Robert Treat (colonial governor of Connecticut), a distant cousin of Thomas Fitch, Robert Treat Paine, and Simeon Baldwin, and part of the Pitkin-Baldwin-Hoar political family of Massachusetts. The Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress also lists him as a great-uncle of Steny Hoyer.
  • He married Marthe Guillon-Verne (born April 10, 1896, Nantes, France) on December 5, 1923; the Political Graveyard notes she was a grandniece of the novelist Jules Verne. They had two sons and two daughters.
  • Religion/affiliations per the Political Graveyard: Episcopalian; member of the Freemasons and the Elks.
  • Siblings named in the Penfield Family Genealogy include sisters Fanny Taylor Baldwin (born 1898) and Phyllis Baldwin (born 1903), and Cynthia Bradley Baldwin (born 1900).
