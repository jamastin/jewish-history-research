Joseph Goebbels (1897–1945)

Joseph Goebbels speaking at the Nuremberg party rally, 1933.

Who he was: Reich Minister of Public Enlightenment and Propaganda from 1933 to 1945 — the man who ran the regime's newspapers, radio, film and rallies, and one of its most radical antisemites. Named Chancellor by Hitler's political testament, he killed himself and his family in Berlin on 1 May 1945.

Key documents

1. The diaries — 1923–1945

Goebbels kept a diary almost daily for over twenty years — handwritten until July 1941, dictated after that. He had the diaries copied onto glass microfiches for safekeeping; that complete copy was found by historian Elke Fröhlich in the former Soviet special archive in Moscow, and the Institut für Zeitgeschichte published her critical edition (Die Tagebücher von Joseph Goebbels, K. G. Saur, 1993–2008), with the full text now also in the De Gruyter database The Diaries of Joseph Goebbels Online (library subscription). The diaries are a self-serving source — Goebbels wrote for posterity — but they are one of the fullest inside records of the regime's decisions and mood, including his meetings with Hitler. - About the edition and its Moscow microfiche source (Oxford, Bodleian Libraries): https://blogs.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/eresources/2012/12/03/new-resource-the-diaries-of-joseph-goebbels-online/ - The database as catalogued by USC Libraries: https://libraries.usc.edu/databases/diaries-joseph-goebbels-online

2. The Sportpalast “total war” speech — 18 February 1943

Goebbels's most famous speech, delivered at the Berlin Sportpalast two weeks after the Stalingrad surrender, to a carefully selected audience. Its core is ten staged questions put to the hall — the fourth being: “Do you want total war? Do you want it, if need be, even more total and radical than we are capable of imagining it today?” In the spoken version he began to say the Jews were being “exterminated,” caught himself mid-word, and the printed text was cleaned up — a small, documented glimpse of the euphemism system at work. - Read the excerpt (GHDI): https://germanhistorydocs.ghi-dc.org/pdf/eng/English93.pdf

Researched 2026-11-21. Flag: the diaries' online edition is subscription-only through libraries; there is no free full-text copy.

In the papers

Newspaper coverage of Goebbels, verified 2026-11-21.

  • Frankfurter Zeitung (Frankfurt, Germany), 20 February 1943, page 7 — a newspaper is itself the surviving source for his most famous speech: the Sportpalast “total war” speech of 18 February 1943 was printed in this German daily two days later, and the GHDI excerpt used in this folder comes from that printing. Its fourth question to the hall: “Do you want total war? Do you want it, if need be, even more total and radical than we are capable of imagining it today?” (Citation from GHDI's source note; the 1943 issue itself is not freely scanned.)
  • The Detroit Jewish News, 29 March 1946, page 3 — Goebbels named from the witness stand at Nuremberg, in the Jewish press's report of Göring's testimony: Göring “told the International Military Tribunal that both Joseph Goebbels and Heinrich Himmler were more rabid Jew-baiters than he.” Scanned page (Bentley Library): https://digital.bentley.umich.edu/djnews/djn.1946.03.29.001/3
  • London Stars and Stripes (the US Army newspaper), 5 May 1945 — three days after Berlin fell, the Army paper was still honestly unsure: a Red Star dispatch indicated no evidence had yet been found in Berlin “to substantiate German statements that Adolf [Hitler], Paul Joseph Goebbels and newly appointed nazi chief of staff Krebs committed suicide.” (His body had in fact been found by Soviet troops; the Western papers did not have it confirmed yet.) Issue listing/OCR (NewspaperArchive): https://starsandstripes.newspaperarchive.com/london-stars-and-stripes/1945-05-05
  • Marlborough Express (New Zealand), 7 May 1945, page 5 — the German version of his death, printed as it arrived by cable: “The German-controlled Prague radio declared that Goebbels, Keitel and Bormann died fighting in the Reich Chancellery in Berlin.” In reality Goebbels had killed himself on 1 May after his children were poisoned — the Prague radio version was the regime's last fiction. Scanned page (Papers Past): https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/MEX19450507.2.34.1
  • Honest note: this sweep did not turn up a verified, freely scanned American day-after report of the February 1943 total-war speech itself — the speech survives through the German printing above, and American coverage is noted here only where a specific item was actually checked.
