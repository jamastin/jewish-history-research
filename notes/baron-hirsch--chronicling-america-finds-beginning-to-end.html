Chronicling America finds — Hirsch, beginning to end (search completed 2026-11-19)

Second loc.gov pass, in three bands. All nine pages were opened on loc.gov and verified in the "Image w/Text" view. These are NEW finds — they do not repeat the six 1891–1896 pages in chronicling-america-finds.md.

Band 1 — Before the philanthropy

1. News and Citizen (Morrisville / Hyde Park, Vt.), February 7, 1889, Image 1 of 4 Headline: "UNPARALLELED PHILANTHROPY. How Baron Hirsch Has Given $4,000,000 in Charity." Quote: "He inherited from his father, the Vienna banker, 40,000,000 florins, which in building Turkish railways and with Ottoman loans, he has decupled." And in Hirsch's own words as quoted: "I am an old man. I am childless. My industry and my enterprises have been eminently successful. With the fortune which I have amassed I hope to raise from the slough of ignorance and despondency, the most helpless, the most miserable race in Europe the Jews of Russia, of Austria, in fact of Southeastern Europe." https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn97067613/1889-02-07/ed-1/?sp=1&q=baron+hirsch+railway

2. New-York Tribune, September 19, 1887, Image 1 of 8 Headline: "BARON HIRSCH'S ORIENTAL RAILWAYS." Quote: "LONDON, Sept. 18.—The sale of Oriental railways by Baron Hirsch to the Ottoman Bank syndicate has been concluded in principle, but the terms are subordinated to the adjustment of a difference between Baron Hirsch and the Porte." https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030214/1887-09-19/ed-1/?sp=1&q=baron+hirsch+railway

Band 2 — Death, will, and Baroness Clara

3. The Watchman and Southron (Sumter, S.C.), April 5, 1899, Image 6 of 8 Headline: "A NOBLE WOMAN DEAD" Quote: "Paris, April 1.—Baroness de Hirsch is dead. Baron Hirsch left an estate of $25,000,000, much of which was bequeathed to charity and afterward dispensed by the Baroness." Also: the Baroness spent $1,500,000 in New York City on charitable work in the three years after his death, beside the annual income of the Baron's $2,400,000 fund. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn93067846/1899-04-05/ed-1/?sp=6&q=baroness+de+hirsch

4. The Topeka State Journal (Topeka, Kan.), May 5, 1897, Image 8 of 8 Headline: "WORKING GIRLS' HOME. Baroness de Hirsch Donates a Sum for One in New York." Quote: "New York, May 5 Plans are under consideration for the erection of the Clare de Hirsch Home for Working Girls from the donation of $1,850,000 by the Baroness de Hirsch for the relief of Russian Hebrews in America." (Printed "Clare"; the home was Clara's.) $200,000 of it was for the building, the rest invested to yield $12,000 a year for running it. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82016014/1897-05-05/ed-1/?sp=8&q=baroness+de+hirsch

5. The Pacific Commercial Advertiser (Honolulu), May 3, 1899, Image 9 of 12 Headline: "$100,000,000 FOR CHARITY." Quote: "LONDON, April 20 Baroness Hirsch leaves a fortune of $124,000,000. She bequeaths $20,000,000 to her relatives." Also: "$1,200,000 to the Hirsch fund in New York, $2,000,000 to the Jewish Colonization Association in London, $120,000 to the Hirsch Institute in Montreal." (One figure in the item — the Board of Guardians in London — was garbled in the scan and is not quoted.) https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85047084/1899-05-03/ed-1/?sp=9&q=jewish+colonization+association

6. The Advocate (Topeka, Kan.), May 6, 1896, Image 9 of 16 One-line note under "NOTES AND COMMENT": "Baron Hirsch willed $1,000,000 to the Prince of Wales." A single-line will note only — not a full will report. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85032018/1896-05-06/ed-1/?sp=9&q=hirsch+will

Band 3 — The work after him

7. The Jewish South (Richmond, Va.), April 9, 1897, Image 3 of 12 Headline: "GIFT OF MILLIONS." Quote: "It is probable that in a very short time the Baron De Hirsch fund in this country will be augmented by a magnificent donation from his widow." The article names Oscar S. Straus, former minister to Turkey, as a trustee of the fund. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn94051168/1897-04-09/ed-1/?sp=3&q=baron+de+hirsch+fund

8. The New Enterprise (Madison, Fla.), January 2, 1902, Image 6 of 10 Headline: "COLONIES IN NEW JERSEY / JEWISH REFUGEES ARE REDEEMING THE WILD LANDS, Rosenhayn and Carmel." Quote: "Woodbine, the best-known of all the colonies, was founded ten years ago, and is directly on the West Jersey and Seashore Road, in the northern part of Cape May County, It contains 200 Jewish and forty Gentile families, Here is located the Baron de Hirsch Agricultural School." https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn95047178/1902-01-02/ed-1/?sp=6&q=woodbine+hirsch

9. The Pacific Commercial Advertiser (Honolulu), March 8, 1903, Image 4 of 8 Headline: "BARON HIRSCH'S EXPERIMENT." Quote: "In 1891 Baron Hirsch was induced by friends in America to found a Jewish colony on the scrub lands of New Jersey, at a point about fifty miles south of Philadelphia. He purchased about thirty thousand acres of land…" And: "At the end of ten years the village of Woodbine has become one of the important settlements of southern New Jersey. It is the only town in the United States entirely under Jewish rule." https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85047084/1903-03-08/ed-1/?sp=4&q=woodbine+hirsch

What came up empty, stated plainly

  • "Hirsch will," 1896–1897: 1,665 hits — "will" is too common; the Advocate one-liner (find 6) was the only usable will item.
  • "Jewish Colonization Association" exact phrase, 1897–1910: only 26 results; the only verified one is Clara's $2,000,000 bequest to the JCA (find 5). No post-1896 JCA report on the Argentine colonies was verified in this pass.
  • No readable page using the nickname "Türkenhirsch" and no standalone page on his horse racing turned up.
  • Some loc.gov searches timed out mid-run; all nine finds above were nonetheless verified in the page text view.
