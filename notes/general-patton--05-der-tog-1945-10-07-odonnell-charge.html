General Patton — Article 5: Der Tog (The Day)

CORRECTION (2026-11-20, after a full read of the actual page)

The full page 6 of the October 7, 1945 Der Tog was read at the Library of Congress on 2026-11-20. The Baldwin "ridiculously ridiculous" item below does NOT appear on page 6 — the page's text contains no "Baldwin," no "Congressman," and no "ridiculously." Where in the issue (or in which paper) the Baldwin item actually ran is unverified: a Chronicling America search for "Baldwin" in this title was blocked by the site's bot check on 2026-11-20. The headline and quote below are kept as originally saved on 2026-11-18, flagged as not confirmed on this page.

What page 6 (the English Magazine page) actually holds on O'Donnell and Patton:

  1. "Is Hitler Dead" — an unsigned editorial attacking O'Donnell: it recalls that FDR presented him with the Nazi Iron Cross, says he "has never forgiven" Roosevelt and "in his Roosevelt hate" did not spare Roosevelt's Jewish friends, and charges that he "invent[ed] a story worthy of another Iron Cross." It then quotes O'Donnell in what it calls his best "Voelkischer Beobachter" style — with a wording variant worth noting: the quote begins "Behind the successful drive to disgrace and remove Gen. George S. Patton..." before the familiar "secret and astoundingly effective might of this republic's foreign-born political leaders" passage (Frankfurter of Vienna; "Dave Niles alias Neyhus"; "the Latvian ex-rabbinical student now know[n] as Sidney Hillman" — OCR reads "know" for "known").
  2. "Just Another Democrat" — mocks Patton's line that "this Nazi thing is just like a Democratic and Republican election fight" and his calling de-Nazification "silly."
  3. "Well Ended. Not Well Done" — on the Harrison Report and camp conditions; Patton paid scant attention until Eisenhower's mid-September visit, when "the heat was turned on."
  4. "How Long, O Lord" — the 600 Jews forced back into Poland, with Pvt. Edward Heilbrun's "My job was sickening… 'Kill me now!'" quote (same account as the New York Post, Oct 2, 1945, item #12 in this folder).

Page OCR is heavily garbled where Yiddish text interleaves; the readings above were checked against the page's headlines and text on 2026-11-20.

  • Newspaper: Der Tog = The Day — New York, N.Y.
  • Date: October 7, 1945 — Image 6 (page 6)
  • Headline: "O'DONNELL CHARGE ON JEWS 'OUT TO GET' GEN. PATTON IS 'RIDICULOUSLY RIDICULOUS', SAY HECKLERS"
  • Link: https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045861/1945-10-07/ed-1/?sp=6&q=Patton+Jews
  • Source: Library of Congress, Chronicling America

Brief statement, in the paper's own words

"It was 'just silly nonsense' and 'ridiculously ridiculous', Congressman Joseph Clark Baldwin said today of the charge by John O'Donnell, Washington columnist for the New York Daily News, that American Jews are 'out to get' Gen. George S. Patton, Jr."

Saved on 2026-11-18 — article #5 from the Patton-and-Jews search, filed in the General Patton subfolder of "where did we go wrong."

Footnote (added 2026-11-20)

  • Joseph Clark Baldwin (1897–1957), the congressman quoted above: Republican U.S. Representative from New York's 17th district, 1941–1947; Harvard 1920; World War I Army officer (French Legion of Honor); former New York Tribune political reporter. Born Joseph Clark Baldwin III, son of Joseph Clark Baldwin Jr. and Fanny Taylor Baldwin; his wife Marthe Guillon-Verne was a grandniece of Jules Verne.
