Name changes — Chronicling America finds, 2026-11-22

The arrival of the first Jews in North America, at New Amsterdam in 1654 (a later drawing).

Verified on the scanned pages at the Library of Congress (loc.gov), from a deep search on 2026-11-22: mentions of Jewish people who changed their names or hid their identity. Quotes are the papers' own words (from LOC's page text; OCR garbles noted where they matter).

1. Grand River Times (Grand Haven, Mich.), September 1, 1852, Image 2

Anecdote headed "Old Rothschild": a man named Isaac "wished to rid himself of the stigma of being known as a Jew. He changed his name... to D' Isaacs," and Rothschild's reply runs: "Ah, sir, Mister Isaacs may change his name, but he cannot change his nose." - https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85026466/1852-09-01/ed-1/?sp=2 - Note: the punchline is a period antisemitic joke — evidence of how name-changing was mocked in print, not a neutral report.

2. Evening Star (Washington, D.C.), February 28, 1909, Image 47

Full article "STYLES IN NAMES." Three cases in one piece: - David Levy → David Levy Yulee: "there settled also in Virginia a Jewish immigrant, David Levy who had come up from the West Indies. Later he went to Florida, studied law, became a planter, was elected delegate to Congress. After serving two terms there as David Levy he changed his name to David Levy Yulee, and as such reentered Congress, this time as United States Senator." - Sidney Lee, "having been born Lazarus, made the change when he entered Oxford, having been advised that he would not otherwise succeed there." - Francis Palgrave, "the son of Meyer Cohen, a Jewish stockbroker of London. Becoming converted to Christianity, he changed his name by royal permission in 1823 to Francis Palgrave." - https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83045462/1909-02-28/ed-1/?sp=47

3. Daily Kennebec Journal (Augusta, Maine), August 6, 1912, page 11

Headline: "WAS COLUMBUS A JEW? Claim Is Put Forth by Rabbi Simon, Washington, D. C." — "His true name, he said, was Christoval Colon, which was a Jewish name. Columbus had changed his name and concealed his true religion, the rabbi declared, because in those days his race was persecuted." - https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn82014248/1912-08-06/ed-1/?sp=11 - This is the closest find to the remembered item — a paper saying someone was known to be Jewish despite a changed name and hidden identity — here it is Columbus, not the boat group.

4. The Phoenix Jewish News (Phoenix, Ariz.), April 8, 1949, Image 3

"Why Does a Man Change His Name? Ambassador Elath Has the Answer" (Milton Friedman, Jewish Telegraphic Agency): "A few weeks ago, a man named Epstein changed his name. His country had changed its name, too... Ambassador Eliahu Elath, who represents the State of Israel here, is the man who changed his name." — the reverse direction: taking a Hebrew name to be known as a Jew, not to hide it. - https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn78001213/1949-04-08/ed-1/?sp=3
