Hitler's Speeches — Audio Recordings You Can Listen To

Adolf Hitler in a trench coat, 1920s (U.S. National Archives).

Researched and link-checked 2026-11-19. Every listening link below was found by searching and opened/verified during the research. Language is marked for each item: German / English / German with English subtitles or transcript.

Start here — the one big collection: "Speeches from Hitler's Germany," Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/Hitler_Speeches One page, free, no account needed. English titles, audio in German. It holds most of the German originals listed in Part 1 (about 100 Hitler items, 1932–1945), plus speeches by Goebbels, Göring and others. Lengths below are the lengths shown in that collection.

Honest limit up front: almost nothing survives from the 1920s. Hitler's early speeches were simply not recorded — recording gear was rare at beer-hall rallies. His first phonograph record ("Hitlers Appell an die Nation") was made for the July 31, 1932 election and published July 20, 1932; the first time he was captured on sound film was June 7, 1931, at Chemnitz. The practical earliest listenable speeches are the 1932 campaign recordings. Anything online claiming to be Hitler's voice from 1920–1927 is not a real recording (see the FAKES section at the end — some are modern AI voice clones, some are readings of speech texts).

Part 1 — German originals (his real voice, in German)

All of these are in the main Internet Archive collection above unless a separate link is given: https://archive.org/details/Hitler_Speeches

1932 — the campaign year (earliest surviving recordings)

  • July 15, 1932 — "Appeal to the Nation" (in the collection: 8m 10s). Language: German. His appeal ahead of the July 31, 1932 Reichstag election — the speech issued as his first phonograph record. Standalone radio-recording copy (12m 38s, page also carries an English translation of the text): https://archive.org/details/19320715AdolfHitlerRedeAmHohenfriedbergAppellAnDieNationRadiomitschnitt12m38s
  • July 21, 1932 — Election rally, Göttingen (4m 57s). Language: German. Campaign speech excerpt.
  • July 27, 1932 — Campaign speech, Eberswalde (10m 48s). Language: German. One of three speeches he gave that day; the film of this speech was reissued in 1933 as "Reichskanzler Adolf Hitler spricht." A subtitled version is in Part 3 below.

1933 — taking power

  • February 1, 1933 — Appeal to the German Volk (13m 19s). Language: German. His first radio address as Chancellor, two days after taking office.
  • February 10, 1933 — Sportpalast speech, Berlin (58m 57s). Language: German. His big Berlin Sports Palace speech opening the March 1933 election campaign. Standalone audio copy: https://archive.org/details/19330210AdolfHitlerRedeImBerlinerSportpalast59m160Kbps An English-subtitled film version is in Part 3 below.
  • March 23, 1933 — Reichstag speech for the Enabling Act. Language: German. The speech asking the Reichstag to hand him dictatorial powers; the collection also has the Hitler–Otto Wels debate from the same sitting (29m) — Wels was the Social Democrat who spoke against him.
  • May 17, 1933 — Reichstag "peace" speech (23m 03s). Language: German.
  • September 1933 — Nuremberg Party Congress excerpts. Language: German. Short pieces in the collection: speech to the youth (2m 08s), to the SA and SS (5m 06s), to the Reich Labor Service (2m 08s).

1934–1938 — Nuremberg rallies and Reichstag speeches

  • July 13, 1934 — Reichstag speech on the SA revolt (68m 22s). Language: German. His justification of the "Night of the Long Knives" purge.
  • September 5, 1934 — Culture speech, Nuremberg Party Congress (21m 20s). Language: German.
  • September 10, 1934 — Closing speech, 6th Party Congress, Nuremberg (10m 07s). Language: German.
  • May 21, 1935 — Reichstag speech introducing general conscription (26m 53s). Language: German.
  • January 30, 1937 — Reichstag speech (19m 33s). Language: German. English translation of a Jan. 30, 1937 Hitler speech is at the Calvin German Propaganda Archive (see Transcripts, below).
  • September 12, 1937 — Speech to the Wehrmacht, Zeppelin Field, Nuremberg (5m 02s). Language: German.
  • September 12, 1938 — Closing speech, Nuremberg Party Congress (34m 30s). Language: German. On the Sudetenland crisis. (Also September 10, 1938: speech to the youth at Nuremberg, 14m 36s.)

1939–1945 — war speeches

  • January 30, 1939 — Reichstag "prophecy" speech, Kroll Opera House, Berlin. Language: German. Sixth anniversary of taking power. This is the speech with the public threat: if "international Jewish financiers" plunged the nations into another world war, the result would be "the annihilation of the Jewish race in Europe." The full speech ran 2–2½ hours and was broadcast live; the copy in the main collection (13m 07s) is excerpt-length. The threat passage was deliberately filmed and recorded for the newsreels (UFA Wochenschau Nr. 439/1939). Playable German audio excerpt of the threat passage, with the full English transcript on the same page — The History Place: https://www.historyplace.com/worldwar2/timeline/threat.htm
  • April 28, 1939 — Reichstag reply to President Roosevelt (97m 49s). Language: German. His long point-by-point answer to Roosevelt's appeal.
  • September 1, 1939 — Reichstag speech announcing war on Poland (35m 03s in the main collection). Language: German. "Since 5:45 a.m. we have been returning fire" — the invasion had begun hours earlier. Standalone copy (38m 33s): https://archive.org/details/19390901AdolfHitlerReichstagsredeKriegserklaerungAnPolen38m33s A US-monitored broadcast copy is also in the Internet Archive "NaziArchive" directory: https://archive.org/download/NaziArchive English full text of the speech (Internet Archive text of the official translation): https://archive.org/stream/AdolfHitlerReturningFireOnPolandSpeech/1939_09_01Hitlerpoland_returningFireae_djvu.txt
  • October 6, 1939 — Reichstag speech after the Poland campaign (Part 1: 25m; Part 2: 29m 34s). Language: German.
  • January 30, 1940 — Sportpalast speech, 7th anniversary (69m 18s). Language: German.
  • July 19, 1940 — Reichstag speech after the Western campaign (95m 22s). Language: German. The "last appeal to reason" speech aimed at Britain.
  • May 4, 1941 — Reichstag speech after the Balkan campaign (70m). Language: German.
  • December 11, 1941 — Reichstag speech declaring war on the USA (20m 51s). Language: German.
  • April 26, 1942 — Reichstag speech (5m 21s in the collection — excerpt). Language: German. His last Reichstag speech.
  • July 20/21, 1944 — Radio address after the assassination attempt (2m 24s in the main collection, plus shorter July 21 items). Language: German. Broadcast around 1:00 a.m. on July 21, 1944, recorded at the Wolf's Lair after the Stauffenberg bomb plot: "you shall hear my voice and know that I personally am unhurt and well." An English-subtitled version is in Part 3. The German radio archive (Deutsches Rundfunkarchiv, Frankfurt) holds the original recording, archive no. DRA 2623118.
  • January 1, 1945 — New Year's address (2m 24s in the collection — excerpt-length). Language: German.
  • January 30, 1945 — LAST RECORDED SPEECH — radio address on the 12th anniversary of the regime (16m 58s). Language: German. ✅ It survives. In the main collection above, and as a standalone file from the British Library Sound Archive on Wikimedia Commons / Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Adolf_Hitler%E2%80%99s_last_speech.ogg?oldformat=true (duration 1,018 seconds, ~17 minutes). English transcript: Jewish Virtual Library, "Adolf Hitler Broadcast on the 12th Anniversary of the National Socialist Regime, January 1945": https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/adolf-hitler-broadcast-on-the-12th-anniversary-of-the-national-socialist-regime-january-1945 Note: his actual last speech (Feb. 24, 1945, party-founding anniversary) was read out by others in Berlin — no recording of his voice delivering it was found.

Part 2 — The Mannerheim recording: Hitler's private, normal voice (1942)

Why this one matters: it is the only known recording of Hitler speaking in an unofficial, conversational tone — not shouting at a rally, just talking, in a normal, even tired-sounding voice. Historians and actors (Bruno Ganz studied it for the film Downfall) use it to hear how he actually sounded off-stage.

  • Date/place: June 4, 1942, Immola, Finland — a railway saloon coach at Marshal Carl Gustaf Emil Mannerheim's 75th birthday visit.
  • How it happened: Thor Damen, a sound engineer for the Finnish broadcaster Yle, was assigned to record the official birthday speeches. He kept recording into the private conversation. After about 11 minutes, Hitler's SS guards spotted the microphone cable, made a cut-throat gesture, and demanded the tape be destroyed. Instead it went to the Finnish State Censors' Office (Kustaa Vilkuna), was returned to Yle in 1957, and was made public a few years later.
  • What he says: he explains the failure of Operation Barbarossa, admits his shock at the scale of Soviet tank and armaments production, and talks about Italian defeats in North Africa, Yugoslavia and Albania, and Romanian oil.
  • Is it real? It was doubted at first because the voice is so soft. Germany's Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) examined it — its head of frequencies, Stefan Gfroerer: "it is very obvious to us that this is Hitler's voice." (Former bodyguard Rochus Misch expressed doubts; the BKA finding is the standing expert verdict.)
  • Language: German (conversation). English subtitles available on the YouTube copy.

Part 3 — English and English-subtitled versions

Plain fact: Hitler never gave a speech in English, and there are no contemporary English dubbed versions of his speeches. What exists in English is: (1) film/video of his real German voice with English subtitles, (2) museum film pages with English transcripts, and (3) printed English translations (see Transcripts below). Anything that sounds like Hitler himself speaking English is modern — see the FAKES section.

  • February 10, 1933 — Sportpalast speech, film with English subtitles. Language: German with English subtitles. The missing parts of the film are re-added as English text translations (at about 9:30, 15:00, 21:17, 28:00): https://archive.org/details/HitlerSpeechSportpalast1933EntireSpeechMissingPartsReAdded Another film copy of the same evening (Goebbels' introduction ~00:24–08:03, Hitler's speech ~11:57–01:06:36): https://archive.org/details/first-speech-as-chancellor-sportpalast
  • July 27, 1932 — Eberswalde campaign speech, with English subtitles. Language: German with English subtitles. The 1932 audio recording with subtitles added: https://archive.org/details/adolf-hitlers-election-campaign-speech-in-eberswalde-27-july-1932
  • July 22, 1932 — Waldenburg campaign speech (film, US Holocaust Memorial Museum). Language: German audio; full English transcript printed on the page. British Movietone footage; in the transcript he promises that "before two or three months pass this terror will be removed if you vote for National Socialists": https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/film/hitler-campaign-speech
  • July 21, 1944 — broadcast after the assassination attempt, with English subtitles. Language: German with English subtitles (video): https://archive.org/details/hitlers-broadcast-speech-rare-after-the-assassination-attempt
  • January 30, 1939 — "prophecy" passage. Language: German audio excerpt + full English transcript on the page (The History Place): https://www.historyplace.com/worldwar2/timeline/threat.htm
  • September 1, 1939 — declaration of war on Poland, with English subtitles circulates as an Internet Archive video item (search the Archive for "Adolf Hitler's Declaration of War Against Poland 1939 — Justice Germans"); the German audio itself is verified in Part 1. Treat subtitled re-uploads as only as good as their uploader — the audio is the same genuine recording.
  • YouTube playlists of subtitled speeches: searches surface playlists titled "Hitler Speeches" collecting the Göttingen (July 21, 1932) and Eberswalde (July 27, 1932) speeches "[English subtitles]" and more, e.g. https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLU8Idp--7OSd5x4r4EVl6jIJs1JkwJgyr — uploader-made subtitles, not official; the underlying recordings match the verified ones above.

Transcripts and English translations (read along while you listen)

  • Calvin University — German Propaganda Archive: English translations of Nazi-era material, including Hitler items (a Jan. 30, 1937 speech on foreign policy; the June 22, 1941 proclamation justifying the invasion of Russia; his last 1945 speech is in the editor's book Landmark Speeches of National Socialism): https://research.calvin.edu/german-propaganda-archive/ww2era.htm
  • Max Domarus, Hitler: Speeches and Proclamations 1932–1945 — the standard scholarly collection; the German radio archive's text of the July 20, 1944 address, for example, is printed in Domarus vol. II from the archive recording (DRA 2623118). Print/library copies; the reference point for exact wording.
  • Speech-by-speech English texts are also attached to several items above: Sept. 1, 1939 (full text link in Part 1), Jan. 30, 1939 (History Place), Jan. 30, 1945 (Jewish Virtual Library), July 22, 1932 (USHMM).

Official archives — where the originals live

  • Deutsches Rundfunkarchiv (DRA), Frankfurt — together with the Bundesarchiv Koblenz, holds the majority of the surviving German radio recordings, including Hitler's July 20, 1944 address (DRA no. 2623118). It has no detailed free online listening catalog — recordings are archival holdings, consulted/ordered rather than streamed. The Internet Archive copies in Part 1 are the practical way to listen.
  • Bundesarchiv — Digitaler Lesesaal (digital viewing room) — the German federal archive's official viewing room holds the newsreel film of speeches such as the Jan. 30, 1939 Reichstag speech (UFA Wochenschau Nr. 439/1939; Deulig Tonwoche Nr. 370/1939), referenced from the Wikipedia article on that speech: https://digitaler-lesesaal.bundesarchiv.de/en/video/8005/684095 and https://digitaler-lesesaal.bundesarchiv.de/en/video/626351/694795 — note: the viewing-room page returned "access denied" to an outside check on 2026-11-19, so treat these as official-archive references, not guaranteed free streaming.
  • US National Archives — Captured German Sound Recordings, Record Group 242: https://www.archives.gov/research/captured-german-records/sound-recordings.html About 525 captured Axis recordings were transferred from June 1947, with 319 more added later — many Hitler speeches, some surviving in no other form. Mostly not online: the page is a descriptive list; copies are ordered, not streamed.
  • Library of Congress — Recorded Sound Reference Center (Madison Building, LM 113) also holds captured/third-reich-era recordings; only a fraction of its audio is online.
  • Yle (Finland) holds the original Mannerheim tape (see Part 2).

⚠️ NOT VERIFIED — fakes, AI voices, and misleading titles

  • "Adolf Hitler AI English Speeches" — Internet Archive: https://archive.org/details/AdolfHitlerAIEnglishSpeeches (and a "V2.0": https://archive.org/details/AdolfHitlerAIEnglishSpeechesV2) These are AI-generated English voice clones laid over the speeches — a modern computer voice made to sound like Hitler, speaking English. Not Hitler's voice. Proof inside their own file lists: they include a "25 Point Programme, 24 Feb 1920" reading and a "Why Are We Antisemites, 13 Aug 1920" — no recordings of any kind exist from 1920; those are texts read aloud by AI.
  • "Adolf Hitler's speeches in English Audio — AUDIOBOOK" — https://archive.org/details/HitlersSpeechesInEnglishAudio Misleading title: despite the name, the audio files are the German recordings; the English part is the accompanying text files (epub/pdf/txt of English translations, including the Feb. 10, 1933 Sportpalast text). Useful for translations — but do not cite it as English audio. Its file list also claims speech MP3s dated 1927 and 1930; those early dates were not independently verified and should be treated with caution (see the 1920s limit at the top).
  • "Hitler's Victory — A Final Appeal for Peace and Sanity, July 19th, 1940" — https://archive.org/details/HitlersVictoryAFinalAppealForPeaceAndSanityJuly19th1940 The German speech underneath is real (July 19, 1940, in Part 1), but the English you hear is a modern narration/voiceover from an advocacy site (justice4germans), edited to ~90 minutes — not a 1940 translation.
  • Viral "Hitler's final speech" clips on social media (e.g. Facebook/Instagram reels of a "final speech in Berlin, Jan. 30, 1945" with a smooth English voice): identified in searches as modern AI-voiced edits over archival footage. The genuine Jan. 30, 1945 recording is the German radio address in Part 1 (British Library copy).
  • Passau, October 27, 1928 — https://archive.org/details/hitler-speech-in-passau-october-1928-german-authentic This is the speech TEXT (book/PDF from the party archive), not audio — do not list it as a 1928 recording. No 1920s speech audio was found anywhere in this research.
