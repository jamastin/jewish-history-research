Agrippa II — the last Herodian king

Written 2026-11-19, following the Berenice deep dive. Sources: Josephus (War, Antiquities, Life), Acts 25–26, Jewish Encyclopedia, modern summaries.

Who he was

  • Marcus Julius Agrippa (Agrippa II), born AD 27/28, son of Agrippa I, great-grandson of Herod the Great, brother of Berenice.
  • Raised at the imperial court in Rome. His father died in AD 44 when Agrippa was about 17 — Claudius's advisors said he was too young to inherit, so his father's whole kingdom went back under direct Roman governors. He never got it back.

The kingdom he actually ruled

  • AD 48/50: given his uncle Herod's small kingdom of Chalcis (Lebanon mountains) — and with it a unique power: supervision of the Jerusalem Temple and the right to appoint and depose the high priest, plus custody connected to the high priest's vestments. He held that Temple authority for the rest of his life, even though Jerusalem itself was never in his kingdom.
  • AD 53: Claudius traded Chalcis for the bigger northern territories of Philip's old tetrarchy — Batanea, Trachonitis, Gaulanitis, around Caesarea Philippi. Nero later added Tiberias and Tarichaeae in Galilee and Julias in Perea.
  • His capital was Caesarea Philippi, which he enlarged and renamed Neronias in Nero's honour. He also spent heavily on Beirut (Berytus).

Friction with Jerusalem

  • He raised a dining room / watchtower on his Jerusalem palace so he could see into the Temple courts; the priests built a high wall to block his view. He and the governor ordered it down; the priests appealed to Nero — and Nero, through Poppaea's intercession, let the wall stand (Josephus, Antiquities 20).

Acts 25–26

  • About AD 59/60 he and Berenice visited the new governor Festus at Caesarea and heard Paul's defence. Paul addressed his argument to Agrippa as the one who knew Jewish law and prophecy; Agrippa's reply (Acts 26:28, KJV): "Almost thou persuadest me to be a Christian."

66 — the speech that failed

  • He was in Alexandria when the Jerusalem crisis broke; he hurried back and delivered a long set-piece speech in Jerusalem (Josephus, War 2.345–404) arguing that Rome was unbeatable and rebellion was suicide. The crowd listened, then turned; he was pelted with stones and withdrew with Berenice.
  • In the war he fought on Rome's side, supplied troops, and was wounded at the siege of Gamala (67).

After the war

  • Rewarded by the Flavians: more territory and praetorian honours in Rome (75), when he travelled there with Berenice.
  • Josephus sent him his history of the war; Agrippa's reply letters (Josephus says 62 of them) praised its accuracy — a king's endorsement Josephus was proud of.

Death — and the end of the line

  • He never married and died childless. The year is disputed: Photius says AD 100, age ~70, in Trajan's third year; modern scholars mostly place it before 93/94. Either way, with him the Herodian dynasty ended — the last Jewish king of antiquity, and his kingdom was folded into the Roman province of Syria.
