The 23 Jews of New Amsterdam — 1654

Picture: images/new-amsterdam-1654-arrival-aja.png — a later illustration captioned "THE ARRIVAL OF THE FIRST JEWS IN NORTH AMERICA, AT NEW AMSTERDAM 1654," from the American Jewish Archives, Cincinnati. It is a later drawing of the event, not a picture from 1654 (none exists).

The story

  • In September 1654, twenty-three Jews — men, women, and children — arrived at New Amsterdam (the Dutch colony that became New York) aboard the French ship St. Catrina (Sainte Catherine).
  • They were refugees from Recife, Brazil. The Dutch had held Recife; when the Portuguese retook it in 1654, the Jews there had to flee or face the Inquisition. On the voyage north their ship was stopped and robbed by Spaniards, and the French ship brought them the rest of the way. They arrived with almost nothing — the ship's captain sued them for the passage money, and the court let him hold their few remaining goods until members of the small Jewish community already in the colony helped pay.
  • Governor Peter Stuyvesant asked the Dutch West India Company for permission to expel them, calling them a "deceitful race" in his letter. The community petitioned back.
  • In April 1655 the Company overruled Stuyvesant and ordered that the Jews be allowed to remain and trade, noting the trouble and expense they had already borne. This group is counted as the founding of the first Jewish community in what became the United States.
  • One earlier arrival is usually named separately: Jacob Barsimson had reached New Amsterdam in August 1654 on the Peereboom, a few weeks before the 23.

Why this sits in Names & Identity

James remembers reading, in an old American newspaper found through Chronicling America, a retelling of an early boat arrival where people said they knew the newcomers were Jewish even though they had hidden or changed their names.

SOURCE FOUND — the newspaper page (verified 2026-11-22)

Evening Star (Washington, D.C.), Sunday, May 21, 1933, page 73 — in "REVIEWS OF THE NEW BOOKS" by Sarah G. Bowerman, reviewing Maud Nathan's Once Upon a Time and Today:

"Her ancestors, Spanish Jews, victims of the persecution of the Inquisition, went to Holland, England, and, finally, to the Dutch colony of New Amsterdam. In September, 1654, a band of 23 Jews came over on the St. Charles. 'Surely the descendants of these Jewish Pilgrims are entitled to consider themselves 100 per cent Americans equally with the descendants of the Mayflower Pilgrims.'"

  • Two honest cautions: (1) The page's text (via LOC's OCR) names the ship "St. Charles" — the name historians usually give is the St. Catrina / Sainte Catherine; the printed word should be checked against the page image before citing the ship's name either way. (2) This page matches the boat, the 23, and September 1654 exactly, but it does not mention Stuyvesant, Recife, or anyone hiding or changing names. The hidden-names element of James's memory was not in this item — that part is still open.
  • Two unverified leads from search snippets, not yet opened: Evening Star, December 27, 1930, Image 6 ("...first group of 23 reached New Amsterdam, now New York, in 1654. They came from Brazil, having left..."); Evening Star, May 4, 1930, Image 90 ("...a company of Jewish refugees from Portugal arrived by way...").
