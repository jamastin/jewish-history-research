Hitler — His Writings & Reading

This is Adolf Hitler's own folder in the “Where Did We Go Wrong” research collection. It gathers what he wrote, what he said on record, and what he read — with the scholarly editions and reliability flags kept visible.

What's in this folder

  • mein-kampf-original-and-translation.md — Mein Kampf, Volumes 1–2: publication facts (Vol. 1, 18 July 1925; Vol. 2, 11 December 1926; Franz Eher Nachfolger, Munich), where to read the original German free in the Institut für Zeitgeschichte critical edition, and the free English Murphy translation (1939), with the Ralph Manheim translation's copyright status stated plainly.
  • hitler-other-writings.md — everything else he wrote, in date order: the Gemlich letter (16 September 1919), the 25-Point Programme (24 February 1920, jointly drafted — not his alone), the “Why We Are Anti-Semites” speech (13 August 1920), the Zweites Buch (written 1928, published 1961), the Domarus speeches collection, the Table Talk (flagged: edited notes, lost originals, Genoud insertions), and his genuine last will and political testament (29 April 1945, Nuremberg record 3569-PS).
  • books-hitler-read.md — the books that influenced him, evidence-graded: about 1,200 volumes of his library survive at the Library of Congress; STRONG evidence for Ford's The International Jew, Chamberlain's Foundations, the Protocols (a forgery), and Karl May; WEAK/MYTH for Schopenhauer and Nietzsche.
  • hitler-audio-speeches.md — recordings you can listen to: the Internet Archive “Speeches from Hitler's Germany” collection, the Mannerheim recording (4 June 1942 — the only known recording of his normal conversational voice), the Sportpalast speech of 10 February 1933 with English subtitles, and his last recorded speech (30 January 1945). Caveats: no 1920s speech audio exists, Hitler never spoke English on record, and AI voice-clones are fakes.

The other major figures

Himmler, Heydrich, Eichmann, Göring, Goebbels and Höss have their own subfolders in the neighbouring nazi-figures/ folder, so this one stays Hitler's alone.

In the papers

Newspaper coverage of Hitler himself — not just German documents. Verified 2026-11-21.

  • The Omaha Evening Bee (Omaha, Nebraska), 12 November 1923 — front-page headline “HITLER CAUGHT IN SOUTH BAVARIA” after the failed Munich beer-hall putsch: “Adolph Hitler, leader of the recent Munich revolt, was arrested today near Lake Staffel, South Bavaria. He offered no resistance.” Verified from a dealer's listing of the original issue (RareNewspapers.com); no free scanned page was found, so this is a citation, not a scan: https://www.rarenewspapers.com/newspaper/569709-beer-hall-putsch-begins-in-1923
  • Wisconsin Rapids Daily Tribune (Wisconsin), 30 January 1933 — front-page headline “HITLER NAMED NEW CHANCELLOR OF GERMANY”; the Berlin wire report begins: “Adolf Hitler, National Socialist party chieftain, was appointed chancellor of Germany today and selected a cabinet which was sworn in immediately.” Scanned page (Internet Archive PDF): https://archive.org/download/wisconsin-rapids-daily-tribune-1933-01-30/wisconsin-rapids-daily-tribune-1933-01-30.pdf
  • The New York Times, 31 January 1933 — front-page headline: “HITLER MADE CHANCELLOR OF GERMANY BUT COALITION CABINET LIMITS POWER; CENTRISTS HOLD BALANCE IN REICHSTAG.” ⚠ Paywalled archive — exact citation given, text not guessed.
  • Nelson Evening Mail (New Zealand), 2 May 1945, page 5 — headline “DEATH OF HITLER.” The paper printed the German radio announcement as it came in: Hitler “had died at his post at the Reich Chancellery in Berlin,” and “Hitler fell for Germany.” Note what the papers could not yet know: the announcement claimed he fell fighting; that he had shot himself was established later. Scanned page (Papers Past, National Library of New Zealand): https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/NEM19450502.2.69
