The Full Picture — the Nazi Records, End to End

What this file is: the wider sweep James asked for on 2026-11-21 — not just the numbers documents, but the whole paper trail: how a state defined a group in law, decided on murder in writing, moved the victims by train and billed for it, recorded the killing as it went, and where all that paper is now. The chain runs: law → decision → trains → camps → reports → archives → trials.

1. The law: the Nuremberg Laws (September 15, 1935)

The legal starting point. Two laws passed at the Nuremberg party rally:

  • Reich Citizenship Law — "A citizen of the Reich is only that subject who is of German or kindred blood." Jews were reduced from citizens to "subjects."
  • Law for the Protection of German Blood and German Honor — marriages and sexual relations between Jews and those of "German or related blood" were forbidden.
  • First Regulation, November 14, 1935 — defined it in writing: "A Jew cannot be a citizen of the Reich" and "A Jew is anyone who descended from at least three grandparents who were fully Jewish by race."

That definition — bureaucratic, genealogical, on paper — is what later decided who was put on the trains. (How American papers reported it the next day is in in-the-papers.md.)

  • The laws in full (GHDI): https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/ghdi:document-1523
  • Nuremberg document texts (Yale Avalon): https://avalon.law.yale.edu/imt/1416-ps.asp

2. The decision trail

Göring's authorization to Heydrich — July 31, 1941. Göring, as head of the Four Year Plan, signed a written order charging Heydrich to prepare "a total solution (Gesamtlösung) of the Jewish question in all the territories of Europe under German occupation" and to submit a plan for "the desired final solution (Endlösung)." (The draft was prepared in Eichmann's office.) This is the written commission for what followed.

  • The document (PDF): https://holocaust.umd.umich.edu/news/uploads/Goring_Heydrich_7_31_1941_web.pdf

The Wannsee Protocol — January 20, 1942. Heydrich convened fifteen senior officials at a villa on Lake Wannsee, Berlin, to coordinate the ministries. The surviving minutes (30 copies were made; only the 16th copy survived the war) contain:

  • A country-by-country table of approximately 11 million Jews "involved in the final solution of the European Jewish question" — including countries Germany had not conquered and never would: England 330,000; neutral Ireland, Sweden, Switzerland, Spain, Portugal and Turkey also listed. General Government 2,284,000; USSR 5,000,000; Ukraine 2,994,684; Hungary 742,800; unoccupied France 700,000. Estonia is listed as already "free of Jews."

  • The method, in the protocol's euphemism: "evacuation of the Jews to the East," Europe to be "combed through from west to east," Jews put to labor in the East where — the protocol says — most would disappear through "natural reduction," and the remainder would have to be "treated accordingly."

  • The protocol (GHDI): https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/the-wannsee-protocol-january-20-1942

  • Facsimile/translation (Yad Vashem, PDF): https://wwv.yadvashem.org/odot_pdf/microsoft%20word%20-%204017.pdf

Himmler's Posen speech — October 4, 1943. The one place a top leader said it plainly, on the record. Addressing SS generals at Posen, Himmler said:

"I am now referring to the evacuation of the Jews, to the extermination of the Jewish people." (German: "Ich meine jetzt die Judenevakuierung, die Ausrottung des jüdischen Volkes.")

He said in the same speech that this was a page of glory that must never be written about publicly. An audio recording of the speech survives at the US National Archives — you can hear Himmler say it. (A second speech, October 6, to party leaders, said the same.)

  • Speech text (GHDI): https://germanhistorydocs.ghi-dc.org/print_document.cfm?document_id=1513

3. The machinery's records

The trains — and the bills. The Reichsbahn (state railway) ran the deportation trains (Sonderzüge, "special trains") as ordinary commercial business. It billed Eichmann's office (RSHA IV-B-4) at the standard third-class group rate: 4 Pfennig per person per track-kilometer; children under ten half fare; children under four free; a group discount at 400+ passengers. The SS charged deportees one-way fare only (their guards' return trips were billed separately) — and in many places Jews were forced to pay the SS for their own deportation, the SS forwarding the money to the railway. Hilberg calculated the railway took in roughly 240 million Reichsmarks from deportation traffic. The paperwork survives: e.g., the letter of July 28, 1942 from Reichsbahn deputy director Ganzenmüller to Himmler's chief of staff Karl Wolff, confirming that since July 22 one train daily was carrying 5,000 Jews from Warsaw to Treblinka, and twice weekly a train of 5,000 from Przemyśl to Bełżec.

Camp administration. Auschwitz was run like an industrial site, and its paperwork shows it: the construction office (Zentralbauleitung) files and crematoria blueprints survived; a construction-office letter of June 28, 1943 reported combined crematoria capacity of 4,756 corpses per day (a capacity figure — the same kind of number the Soviet commission later misused as a death count; see auschwitz-numbers.md). Zyklon B was procured through the firms Degesch and Tesch & Stabenow with ordinary order and delivery paperwork. The SS economic main office (WVHA, under Oswald Pohl) ran the camp system as an economic enterprise, generating correspondence on prisoner labor and looted property. Registration records and death books exist for registered prisoners only — the majority of victims, gassed on arrival, were never registered (see auschwitz-numbers.md).

Ghetto records survive from both sides: German administration files, and records created by the victims themselves — most famously Warsaw's Oyneg Shabes archive, buried in milk cans and recovered after the war.

The Stroop Report — Warsaw, 1943. SS General Jürgen Stroop's official report to Himmler on destroying the Warsaw ghetto, leather-bound, illustrated with 53 photographs, titled "The Jewish Quarter of Warsaw Is No More!" It contains a summary, 31 daily reports (April 20 – May 16, 1943), and Stroop's own final tally:

"Total number of Jews dealt with 56,065, including both Jews caught and Jews whose extermination can be proved."

— of whom about 7,000 were killed in the ghetto during the operation and 6,929 were "destroyed by transport to Treblinka," with an estimated 5,000–6,000 more killed by fire and explosions. It was prosecution evidence at Nuremberg and again at the 1947 WVHA trial. Copies survive in Warsaw and at the US National Archives; the report is on UNESCO's Memory of the World register (2017).

Image file

Image file — one of the most reproduced photographs of the war; the boy with raised hands in the foreground became its best-known face.

4. Where the records live now

  • Arolsen Archives (Bad Arolsen, Germany) — the largest collection on earth: ~30 million documents on ~17.5 million people — camp records, forced-labor files, displaced-persons files — with a Central Name Index of ~50 million references. Sealed for decades, opened to researchers in 2007; an online archive launched in 2019 and keeps growing. UNESCO Memory of the World. https://arolsen-archives.org/
  • Bundesarchiv (Berlin-Lichterfelde and Koblenz) — the German federal archive: the regime's ministry files and the Bildarchiv photo collection.
  • Yad Vashem (Jerusalem) — documents plus the Central Database of Shoah Victims' Names (see historians-totals.md).
  • USHMM (Washington) — holds a full digital copy of the Arolsen collection and its own captured-records holdings.
  • NARA (US National Archives, College Park) — Record Group 238 holds the Nuremberg records, including the Korherr Report original (NO-5194) and the recording of Himmler's Posen speech. Captured German records were microfilmed; most originals were later returned to Germany.

5. What was destroyed — and what survived anyway

The regime tried to erase the record, and partly succeeded. Being honest about that is part of the evidence:

  • February 1943: Himmler visited Treblinka and ordered the camp's records, crematoria and other traces destroyed. The three Operation Reinhard camps were dismantled in late 1943 and their records almost entirely destroyed. The Höfle Telegram survives only because the British intercepted the radio signal — the SS's own copy is gone (see hofle-telegram.md).
  • Aktion 1005 (1942–44): a special unit under Paul Blobel exhumed and burned bodies at mass-shooting sites across the East to erase the evidence of the Einsatzgruppen murders.
  • January 1945: the Auschwitz SS burned camp records and dynamited the crematoria before evacuating. What survived — death books, construction files — survived because it was seized or overlooked.
  • The Jäger Report survived in exactly one of its five copies, in a Moscow archive.
  • Despite all of it, what remains — captured ministry files, SS statistics, railway bills, camp construction records, intercepted signals, the perpetrators' own reports — is the mountain the totals in this folder are built from. The surviving record is a floor, not a ceiling.

6. The postwar paper trail

  • Nuremberg (1945–49). The prosecution deliberately built its case on the regime's own captured files, organized into document series (PS, NG, NO, EC…). The Einsatzgruppen trial (1947–48) rested on the squads' own situation reports — the defendants were convicted substantially out of their own paperwork. The Wannsee Protocol, the Göring authorization, the Posen speech recording and the Stroop Report were all prosecution exhibits.
  • The Eichmann trial (Jerusalem, 1961). Fifteen years later the documentary record was presented again, alongside more than a hundred survivor witnesses; Eichmann's own recorded 1957 interviews (the Sassen tapes), in which he boasted of his role, were played in evidence. How the papers covered it is in in-the-papers.md.
  • Höss's account — flagged as testimony. Rudolf Höss, Auschwitz's commandant, gave an affidavit (May 14, 1946) claiming 2 million Jews gassed at Auschwitz plus half a million more dead — and wrote a memoir in Polish custody (1946–47). Flag: Höss is a perpetrator witness, not a neutral record; his totals were inflated (he attributed the 2.5 million figure to Eichmann), and historians do not use them. His memoir is corroborated on how the camp operated — the machinery, the procedures — which is what it is evidence for. The Auschwitz death toll (~1.1 million) comes from the transport records, not from Höss.
