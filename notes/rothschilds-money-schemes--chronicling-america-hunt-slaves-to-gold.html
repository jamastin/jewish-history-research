Hunt for the "slaves to gold" passage — Chronicling America / book search

Date of hunt: 2026-11-19 Target passage (album item M062, transcribed from James's album page; spelling "Rothchilds" as printed):

"…will pledge our lives and honor to its support," say the people. / The Greenback is the new departure. It is America for America. It is the American financial policy. It is the people for the people. It is the nation saying to all its enemies, 'hands off, this is our money. Gold is good, but it is scarce, foreigners control it. Greenbacks are better. They are from the people for the people. They are the nation for the nation. They are Americans.' … To be free, a nation must control its own money. To be slaves to gold is to be slaves to the Rothchilds and German Jews. With Greenbacks for our currency and a money policy of our own free from all banking interference, (and above and beyond all foreign interference) which shall fur…

Bottom line (2026-11-19): NOT verified found anywhere. No citation is claimed. The passage's exact phrases appear in no openly indexed full text searched — not on the general web, not in any of the 11 Greenback/Populist-era books read in full, not in the sources ruled out earlier. The one place it can still be hiding that this hunt could not search directly is inside Chronicling America's own OCR index, which is only searchable on the loc.gov site itself (see "Access blocks" and "What remains" below). A live-browser check of Chronicling America is the remaining route.

Method and tools

  • Web search (browser.search) and page text fetch (browser.open + browser.find) only. This agent has no live browser, so it cannot operate the Chronicling America search interface directly.
  • Full texts of candidate books were opened on archive.org (djvu.txt) and searched line-by-line with browser.find for: "new departure", "Rothchild", "German Jews", "slaves to gold", "America for America", "hands off".

What the evidence supports

  • The passage's exact phrases appear nowhere in general web indexes, Google Books results, HathiTrust results, or any full text opened. That is consistent with three possibilities: (a) it lives in a Chronicling America newspaper whose OCR is searchable only inside CA (CA page OCR is largely not exposed to outside search engines); (b) it is in a book/pamphlet that has never been full-text indexed; or (c) CA's OCR garbles the key words so even CA's own phrase search needs loose/proximity variants.
  • The album crop (M062) shows no headline, folio, or masthead, so the source cannot be identified from the image alone. The bold display paragraph and the narrative frame ("…say the people") fit either a newspaper editorial set piece or a passage inside a movement book/history.
