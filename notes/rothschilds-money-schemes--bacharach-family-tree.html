The Bacharach Family Tree — origins, marriages, name changes, and where the branches led

Researched for James, 2026-11-19. Sources: Encyclopaedia Judaica "Bacharach" entry (via encyclopedia.com); Lars Menk, A Dictionary of German-Jewish Surnames (via the Bacharach Surname DNA Project); the Rothschild Archive (family's own archive); JewishGen Rav-SIG surnames of rabbinical families; Wikipedia (Yair Bacharach; Mayer Amschel Rothschild); Geneanet/peerage genealogies for the Frankfurt line. Genealogy websites vary in detail; the lines below are the ones the sources agree on. Flagged where a claim comes from a single genealogy site.

1. Where the name comes from — and what they were called before it

  • Bacharach is a town on the Rhine in Germany. Jewish families who came from that town took its name when they settled elsewhere. Lars Menk's dictionary dates the surname's adoption: Worms 1449, Mainz 1455, Frankfurt am Main 1516. Gottschalk of Bacharach is mentioned in Frankfurt as early as 1391; Ephraim Gumbrecht Bacharach in 1457; Menahem (Man) Bacharach was rabbi in Worms 1506–1520.
  • Before the surname, these families were known the Frankfurt way: by patronymics ("son of") and by house signs in the Judengasse. In the records the same Bacharach kin appear as: zum Hahn (the Rooster), zur roten Rose (the Red Rose), zum roten Schild (the Red Shield), zum Bauer, zur Leiter, zur Reuse, zum Waag, zum Buchsbaum, zum Hasen, zum Kessel, and the Knoblauch branch (named for the cloth and silk merchant Mannlin zum Knoblauch; a Bacharach branch in Frankfurt from about 1516).
  • One branch carries a second town name: Isaac Bachrach zu Lauchheim — Lauchheim is a town in Württemberg. Wives are often recorded by their home towns the same way: Brendel aus Burgau, Gutlin Boppard (of Boppard, also on the Rhine).
  • Spelling variants of the one name: Bacharach, Bachrach, Bacherach, Backerack, Bakhrakh.
  • Honest caveat (Bacharach DNA Project): because the name is a town name, more than one unrelated Jewish family could have taken it; not every Bacharach in the records is provably one family. The two great lines below are the documented ones.

2. The Frankfurt line — Bacharach becomes Rothschild

  • Uri Feibesch zu Bacharach (b. ~1475) — the earliest ancestor in the Frankfurt genealogies.
  • His son Elchanan "zur roten Rose" (1496–1550) married Fogele zu Worms, daughter of Rabbi Meir zu Worms (1489–1540) and Gutlin Worms — a Worms rabbinical family married in at the very start.
  • Their son Isaac (Isaak) Elchanan Bacharach, "zum Hahn, zum roten Schild" (1528–1588) married Esther (recorded as zur Leiter / zur Reuse; d. 1609). He is the first man known to use the name Rothschild, taken from the Red Shield house in the Judengasse (built 1567). This is the same fact the Rothschild Archive gives: the name comes from the house, not from an earlier surname.
  • His son Moses Isaac Rothschild (c. 1560–1635), "Mosche zum roten Schild," married Gutchen (Gitchen) Naftali.
  • The line runs down through Herz Moses → Kalman Herz → Moses Kalman → Amschel Moses Rothschild (d. 1755), who married Schönche Lechnich (d. 1756) — the Lechnich family; her parents are given in the genealogies as Mayer Lechnich and Feyga Grotwol.
  • Their son Mayer Amschel Rothschild (1744–1812), founder of the banking dynasty, married Gutle Schnapper (1753–1849) on 29 August 1770 — daughter of Wolf Salomon Schnapper, of the Frankfurt court-factor Schnapper family.
  • Mayer Amschel and Gutle's children and who they married (the marriages that tie the Rothschilds to the other great banking families):
  • Schönche Jeannette → Benedikt Moses Worms (the von Worms family)
  • Amschel Mayer → Eva Hanau
  • Salomon Mayer → Caroline Stern (Frankfurt banking family)
  • Nathan Mayer → Hannah Barent Cohen, daughter of Levi Barent Cohen (Dutch-born London financier)
  • Isabella → Bernhard Juda Sichel
  • Babette → Siegmund Leopold Beyfus
  • Carl Mayer → Adelheid Herz
  • Henriette → Abraham Montefiore (brother of Sir Moses Montefiore)
  • James Mayer → Betty de Rothschild, his own niece (the family married cousins to keep the bank's capital inside)
  • A brother-line of the same Frankfurt Bacharachs (per the Reis/Wolf/Samuel family research site): Isaac Bachrach zu Lauchheim's son Mendlen (Menachem) Isaac Lauchheim-Bacharach (c. 1535–1599) — brother of Elchanan zur roten Rose — married Edel Weissenberg (daughter of Moses Weissenberg and Gutlin Boppard). Their son Samuel Mendel Bacharach (c. 1548–1628) married Henlen Bloch; their daughter Edel Bacharach married Simon Wolf Oppenheim — making the Bacharachs ancestors of Rabbi David Oppenheim of Prague through the Oppenheim, Wohl, Behrens-Cohen, Frankel-Teomim, Bondi, Reis, and Samuel families.

3. The rabbinical line — Worms, Prague, Moravia

  • Abraham Samuel Bacharach (c. 1575–1615), rabbi of Worms, founded in Bohemia per the Encyclopaedia Judaica. He married Eva (Havvah) Bacharach (1580–1651), a learned woman in her own right and granddaughter of the Maharal of Prague, Judah Loew ben Bezalel — Eva's father was Isaac Katz, the Maharal's son-in-law. So this Bacharach line marries straight into the most famous rabbinical family in Europe.
  • Eva died in Sofia in 1651 on her way to Eretz Israel — the family's earliest Palestine connection: she was travelling to settle in the Land of Israel and never arrived.
  • Their son Moses Samson Bacharach (1607–1670), rabbi of Worms, married a daughter of R. Isaac b. Phoebus, chief rabbi of Moravia; his second wife was Phega (through whom his son became step-brother to the physician Tobias Cohn).
  • Their grandson Yair Chayim Bacharach (1638–1702), the Chavot Yair, rabbi of Worms like his father and grandfather; born in Lipník, Moravia. His book's title honours his grandmother Eva ("Havvah").

4. The eastern branch — Poland-Lithuania (today's Belarus/Poland), NOT the Rus'

  • A Bacharach family reached the Polish-Lithuanian east: in Ruzhany (today Belarus), two sons of Rabbi Joseph Solomon Bachrach — Israel and Tobias — were martyred in a blood libel on 19 September 1659; Rabbi Tuviah Bachrach was martyred there the same year. Tobias's descendants lived at Tykocin (Tiktin), Poland. The Zak / Zaks family name (from Zerae Qoydesh, "holy seed") descends from those Ruzhany martyrs.
  • The tradition recorded by the Bacharach DNA Project: the Ruzhany family was thought to have originated in the Frankfurt–Worms area — i.e., the movement was west → east, from the Rhineland into Poland-Lithuania, in the centuries when German Jews migrated east (the same broad migration that carried Yiddish east).
  • Direct answer to the Rus'/Ukraine question: nothing in the record makes the Bacharachs Rus', Kievan, Ukrainian, or Khazar in origin. The name is a German Rhine town; every early record is Worms, Mainz, Frankfurt, Prague, Moravia. A branch later lived in the eastern lands (today's Belarus and Poland, at the edge of Ukraine's historical region), but they got there from Germany.

5. Palestine

  • 1651: Eva Bacharach dies at Sofia travelling to Eretz Israel (above).
  • 1882 onward: the Rothschild branch — Baron Edmond de Rothschild of the Paris house (son of James Mayer) funded and administered the early Jewish settlements in Ottoman Palestine (Rishon LeZion, Zikhron Ya'akov, and others) — the same story that runs alongside Baron Hirsch's colonies in James's Hirsch folder, though Hirsch worked in Argentina and Edmond in Palestine. The Rothschilds did not come from Palestine; in the 1880s their money went there.

6. The linking family names (all of them)

Bacharach · Rothschild · Worms · Schnapper · Lechnich · Grotwol · Stern · Cohen (Barent Cohen) · Sichel · Beyfus · Hanau · Herz · Montefiore · Oppenheim · Bloch · Weissenberg · Boppard · Wohl · Behrens-Cohen · Frankel-Teomim · Bondi · Reis · Samuel · Katz (the Maharal of Prague's family) · Phoebus · Lauchheim · Burgau · Naftali · Zak/Zaks (the Ruzhany line).

7. Bottom line

One Rhineland root (the town of Bacharach), first surnames at Worms/Mainz/Frankfurt in the 1400s–1500s, house-sign names before that (Red Rose, Red Shield, Rooster, and the rest), two great documented lines — Frankfurt bankers who became the Rothschilds, and Worms/Prague rabbis who married into the Maharal's family — an eastern offshoot in Poland-Lithuania by the 1600s, and a Palestine connection that runs from Eva's fatal 1651 journey to Edmond's settlements in the 1880s. No Rus', no Khazar, and no Ukraine in the family's origins; those lands appear only as places a branch later lived.
