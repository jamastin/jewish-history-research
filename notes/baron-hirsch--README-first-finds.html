Baron Hirsch — first finds

Folder created 2026-11-19, inside "where did we go wrong."

Who he is

Baron Maurice de Hirsch (Moritz Freiherr von Hirsch auf Gereuth), born December 9, 1831, Munich; died April 21, 1896, at Ógyalla, Hungary. German Jewish financier (railroad money — Turkish, Russian, Austrian lines) who turned to philanthropy. In 1891 he put up $2,400,000 for the Baron de Hirsch Fund in the United States for Jewish immigrants from Russia and Romania, and founded the Jewish Colonization Association (JCA) the same year to settle Russian Jews as farmers — above all in Argentina (Moisés Ville, Colonia Mauricio, Clara, and others), also Brazil, Canada, and the U.S. (Woodbine, New Jersey). His fortune was estimated around $100 million by 1890. After his only son died in 1887 he is quoted: "My son I have lost, but not my heir; humanity is my heir."

His own words

  • North American Review, July 1891 ("My Views on Philanthropy"): "What I desire to accomplish, what, after many failures, has come to be the object of my life, and that for which I am ready to stake my wealth and my intellectual powers, is to give a portion of my companions in faith the possibility of finding a new existence, primarily as farmers and also as handicraftsmen, in those lands where the laws and religious tolerance permit them to carry on the struggle for existence as noble and responsible subjects of a humane government."
  • Interview with the New York World, 1892, on the Argentine colonies: "These exiles are so poor that if they are given the means to work and they are taught to work the land, so that the harvest would be sufficient to feed their families and earn some profit as well, I have no doubt that they will make good use of the opportunity."
  • The 1891 trust deed of his American fund, as reported in the papers at the time: he had "observed with painful interest the suffering and destitution of the Hebrews dwelling in Russia and Rumania where they are oppressed by severe laws and unfriendly neighbors, and have determined to contribute to the relief of such of my brethren in race who have emigrated or shall emigrate from these inhospitable countries to the Republic of the United States of America." (Wording as carried in later summaries of the February 1891 reporting — verify against an original paper before quoting as exact.)

Newspaper finds

  1. Ashburton Guardian (New Zealand), February 9, 1891, page 3 — "HEBREWS IN EUROPE. Vienna, February 8. Baron Hirsch has given twelve million francs (£430,000) to promote Hebrew emigration to America." https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/AG18910209.2.14.2
  2. The Press (Christchurch, New Zealand), June 15, 1896, page 3 — on his death: "Baron Hirsch died this week. He succumbed to apoplexy at one of his vast country residences on the Continent. He was an extraordinary man, and perhaps the most liberal in charitable deeds of any man of our time. He made his immense wealth by driving keen bargains and by shrewd speculations, but when he acquired millions he spent millions… He was never on intimate terms with the Rothschilds, and despised those of his own countrymen who were grasping and greedy in amassing money… Many poor people will miss Baron Hirsch." The same column reports his wealth "totalled £30,000,000." https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/CHP18960615.2.12
  3. Otago Witness (New Zealand), July 2, 1896 — "Talk of the Day" retells how Hirsch was blackballed from the French Jockey Club "in consequence of the prejudice existing in Paris against the Jews," and how he retaliated by buying the ground the Jockey Club's premises stood on. https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/newspapers/OW18960702.2.93.1
  4. New York Herald, April 22, 1896 (via the Center for Research Libraries scan) — cable from Vienna, April 21: "Baron Maurice de Hirsch, the great financier and philanthropist, died last night on his estate near Presburg, Hungary, from a stroke of apoplexy." (Scan OCR is rough; the passage is legible in the page image.) https://digitalcollections.crl.edu/record/418043/files/011.pdf

Notes / next steps

Note: the papers disagree on the fund amount — Tribune and The Journal print $2,500,000, Foster's Daily Democrat prints $2,400,000.
  • Charles Taze Russell wrote to Hirsch and Edmond de Rothschild on August 18, 1891 urging them to fund buying Palestine instead of emigration elsewhere; Hirsch did not take it up — he considered Palestine unrealistic at the time.
