The Einsatzgruppen Reports — and the Jäger Report (1941)

What they are: the Einsatzgruppen were SS mobile killing units that followed the German army into the Soviet Union from June 1941 and murdered Jews, Roma, communists and others in mass shootings. They reported their work to Berlin in regular written situation reports (Ereignismeldungen — "event reports"), with numbers. Those reports survived the war in captured German files and were used at Nuremberg and at the Einsatzgruppen trial (1947–48), where the defendants were the reports' own authors and recipients.

The Jäger Report — one unit, five months, itemized

The single most detailed surviving example: the report of SS-Standartenführer Karl Jäger, commander of Einsatzkommando 3 (part of Einsatzgruppe A), dated December 1, 1941, titled "Complete tabulation of executions carried out in the Einsatzkommando 3 zone up to December 1, 1941."

  • 112 executions in 71 locations in Lithuania (and parts of Latvia and Belarus), listed by date and place, with victims broken down as Jewish men, women, and children, plus communists, the mentally ill, and others.
  • Running total: 137,346 people murdered between July 2 and November 25, 1941 — by this one commando and its Lithuanian auxiliaries. On 17 days the daily toll exceeded 2,000. (Some transcriptions give 137,348 — a two-person discrepancy in the published literature; the report's own table totals 137,346.)
  • Sample entries, in the report's own categories: Kaunas, Fort VII, July 6, 1941 — 2,514 Jews. Rokiškis, August 15–16 — "3,200 Jews, Jewesses, and Jewish Children." Panevėžys, August 23 — 1,312 men, 4,602 women, 1,609 children = 7,523.
  • On February 9, 1942, Jäger updated the tally to 138,272, of whom 136,421 were Jews.
  • Jäger's own conclusion, verbatim: "I can confirm today that the goal of solving the Jewish problem for Lithuania has been achieved by EK 3. In Lithuania, there are no more Jews, except for the Arbeitsjuden ["labor Jews"] incl. their families." He added that he had wanted to kill those too, but the civil administration and the army forbade it.
  • What it does not include (stated in the scholarship, and it matters for honesty): killings by Einsatzkommando 2 in the Šiauliai area (~46,000), border-area massacres, and some Vilnius massacres are missing — the report is a floor for its own zone, not a complete count even of Lithuania.
  • The report was prepared in five copies; one survives, held by the Russian State Military Archive in Moscow. It surfaced publicly in the 1960s.

Read the report (full English translation): https://kehilalinks.jewishgen.org/rokiskis/jager.html

Image file — the map Stahlecker attached to his report to Heydrich; the US Holocaust Memorial Museum describes the same map as Nuremberg evidence, its legend giving "the estimated number of Jews still on hand [as] 128,000."

The shooting totals overall

  • Historian Raul Hilberg's estimate: the Einsatzgruppen, related SS and police agencies, and their foreign auxiliaries murdered more than two million people, including 1.3 million Jews, between 1941 and 1945.
  • The US Holocaust Memorial Museum's broader category — all "mass shooting operations and related massacres," carried out in more than 1,500 cities, towns and villages across occupied eastern Europe, by the SS, police battalions, the army and local collaborators together — comes to about 2 million Jewish victims.
  • Individual massacres inside those reports are independently documented, e.g. Babi Yar outside Kyiv, September 29–30, 1941: the Einsatzgruppe's own report gives 33,771 Jews shot in two days.
