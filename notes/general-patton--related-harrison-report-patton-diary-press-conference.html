Related items — the Harrison Report, Patton's diary, and the September 22 press conference

Saved on 2026-11-18, in the General Patton subfolder of "where did we go wrong." These three are the background documents the fall 1945 newspaper stories kept referring to.

1. The Harrison Report (August 24, 1945)

Earl G. Harrison, sent by President Truman to inspect the displaced persons camps, reported on conditions for Jewish survivors. Its most quoted line:

"As matters now stand, we appear to be treating the Jews as the Nazis treated them except that we do not exterminate them. They are in concentration camps in large numbers under our military guard instead of S.S. troops."

Harrison also wrote that many Jewish displaced persons were "living under guard behind barbed-wire fences, in camps of several descriptions (built by the Germans for slave-laborers and Jews), including some of the most notorious of the concentration camps." The report was published in the New York Times on September 30, 1945. The camps in Patton's Bavarian zone were singled out as particularly badly run, and the contrast with Patton's position contributed to his relief from command.

2. Patton's diary, September 15, 1945 — his answer to Harrison

Not a newspaper item: Patton's diary was published only in 1974, in The Patton Papers. His words on the Harrison report:

"One of the chief complaints is that the DP (Displaced Person) are kept in camps under guard. Of course Harrison is ignorant of the fact that if they were not kept under guard they would not stay in the camps, would spread over the country like locusts and should eventually have to be rounded up after quite a few of them had been shot and quite a few Germans murdered and pillaged."

"Evidently the virus started by Morgenthau and Baruch of a Semitic revenge against all Germans is still working. Harrison and his associates indicate that they feel German civilians should be removed from houses for the purpose of housing Displaced Persons."

His September 17, 1945 diary entry, after attending Yom Kippur services at Feldafing with Eisenhower, is also in The Patton Papers: "We entered the synagogue, which was packed with the greatest stinking bunch of humanity I have ever seen... The smell was so terrible that I almost fainted and actually about three hours later lost my lunch as the result of remembering it."

3. The September 22, 1945 press conference

Asked why Nazis were still holding government jobs in Bavaria, Patton compared Nazi party membership to joining a party at home. The New York Times headlined his answer as:

"The Nazi thing is just like a Democrat-Republican fight."

(As later accounts give his fuller phrasing: "this Nazi question... very much like a Democratic and Republican election fight.") In his diary that night he wrote: "There is a very apparent Semitic influence in the press. They are trying to do two things: first, implement communism, and second, see that all businessmen of German ancestry and non-Jewish antecedents are thrown out of their jobs."

This press conference, together with the Harrison report controversy, led to Patton being relieved as military governor of Bavaria and given the Fifteenth Army instead.
