Baron Maurice de Hirsch — beginning to end (life arc)

Sources: Wikipedia/Encyclopaedia Britannica (1922), Encyclopaedia Judaica via encyclopedia.com, Jewish Review of Books on Lehmann's biography The Baron, Theodore Norman, An Outstretched Arm: A History of the Jewish Colonization Association (1983, full text on archive.org), Jewish Encyclopedia entry on Clara de Hirsch.

The beginning

  • Born Moritz von Hirsch auf Gereuth, December 9, 1831, Munich, Bavaria — into a family of Jewish court bankers. His grandfather Jacob was the first Jewish landowner in Bavaria, ennobled in 1818; his father Joseph was banker to the Bavarian king and made a baron in 1869. His mother Caroline was descended from Rabbi Samson Wertheimer, the famous Viennese court Jew.
  • Sent to Brussels at 13 for schooling; went into business at 17 (1848) at the bank of Bischoffsheim & Goldschmidt.
  • June 28, 1855: married Clara Bischoffsheim, daughter of his senior partner. Clara had been her father's chief secretary and became her husband's — she was inside all his business and charity work from the start.

The fortune — the "Türkenhirsch" (the Turkish Hirsch)

  • Struck out on his own in railway finance — lines in Russia, Hungary, Austria.
  • 1869: the Ottoman concession. His former partner Langrand-Dumonceau's empire collapsed in a fraud scandal; out of the wreckage Hirsch obtained the concession to build the first ~2,000 km of railways in the Ottoman Empire, linking Constantinople with the European network — the line that became the Chemins de fer Orientaux, the Orient Express route. First train out of Constantinople: January 1871, before massive crowds. He placed nearly two million Ottoman railway bonds on 28 stock exchanges at once.
  • Also sugar and copper. Fortune estimated at ~$100,000,000 by 1890 — among the five richest individuals in Europe. Sold the Ottoman railroads in 1890 to a consortium including Deutsche Bank.
  • Famous in England for horse racing; his 1892 racing winnings alone (£40,000) went to London hospitals.

The turn to philanthropy

  • 1877: ran his own relief service for victims of the Russo-Turkish War.
  • 1887: his only son Lucien died — the turn. His quoted words: "My son I have lost, but not my heir; humanity is my heir."
  • 1887–88: offered the Russian government $10,000,000 for Jewish schools on the one condition of no distinction of race or religion (the Foster's Daily Democrat obituary, April 22, 1896, prints this).
  • 1891: founded the Jewish Colonization Association in London with £2,000,000 capital; added £7,000,000 more in 1892. Of the 20,000 JCA shares, Hirsch personally held 19,991. Also 1891: the Baron de Hirsch Fund in the U.S. ($2,500,000) for Jewish immigrants from Russia and Romania.
  • His own statement of purpose, North American Review, July 1891: to give his "companions in faith the possibility of finding a new existence, primarily as farmers and also as handicraftsmen."
  • June 1895: Theodor Herzl came up the marble staircase of his Paris house at 2, Rue de l'Élysée to pitch political Zionism; Hirsch listened but stayed with his own colonization plan.
  • Kurt Grunwald's calculation (in Türkenhirsch): Hirsch's charitable donations in life and by will exceeded $100 million — roughly $2.5 billion in today's purchasing power.

The end

  • Died April 21, 1896, at Ógyalla, Hungary (the American papers said "near Komorn" / "near Presburg"), of apoplexy / heart disease.
  • He left Clara sole administrator and residuary legatee of the whole fortune.

After him — Clara finishes the work (1896–1899)

  • Clara ran the office from their house on the Champs-Élysées, secretaries around her, early morning to late night. The Jewish Encyclopedia says it was she who had "gently guided his interests toward philanthropy" all along.
  • In her three years of widowhood she gave away more than $15,000,000: $1,000,000 to America a year after his death to relieve the New York ghetto by moving immigrants to the country; $150,000 for the Baron de Hirsch Trade School building in New York; $200,000 to build the Clara de Hirsch Home for Working Girls, endowed with $600,000 more; a $700,000 pension fund for the officials of her husband's Oriental railways; $500,000 each to the Pasteur Institute and the Philanthropic Society of Paris; bureaus in Vienna and Budapest.
  • Died April 1, 1899, in Paris. Her will left a further $10,000,000 to endow the foundations, including 10,000,000 francs to the Jewish Colonization Association. Her plan had been to give away essentially the entire fortune; death stopped her before it was done.
  • Between them, their benefactions were estimated at £18,000,000 in their lifetimes (Britannica), and on Clara's death the JCA's capital rose to £11,000,000.
