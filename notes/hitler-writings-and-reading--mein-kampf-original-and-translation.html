Mein Kampf — the original German book and a readable English translation

Cover of an early English edition of Mein Kampf. Source: Wikimedia Commons

Researched 2026-11-19. Every link below was opened or returned by search on that date. Where a link could not be fully verified, that is said plainly.

1. The original book — exact details

  • Author: Adolf Hitler
  • Title: Mein Kampf ("My Struggle")
  • Volume 1 — Eine Abrechnung ("A Reckoning") — published 18 July 1925
  • Volume 2 — Die nationalsozialistische Bewegung ("The National Socialist Movement") — published 11 December 1926 (some listings give 10 December 1926; the volume is dated 1927 in some catalogues)
  • Publisher: Franz Eher Nachfolger (Franz Eher Nachf. Verlag), Munich — the Nazi Party's own publishing house, run by Max Amann
  • Original working title: Viereinhalb Jahre (des Kampfes) gegen Lüge, Dummheit und Feigheit — "Four and a Half Years (of Struggle) Against Lies, Stupidity and Cowardice." Amann is credited with shortening it to Mein Kampf.
  • How it was written: Volume 1 was dictated/written largely in Landsberg prison in 1924, after the failed November 1923 putsch. Volume 2 was written mostly after his release, at Obersalzberg.
  • Size: the two volumes together run about 720–781 pages depending on the edition. Over 12.5 million copies had been printed by 1945 (Historisches Lexikon Bayerns / Bavarian State Library).

Copyright status — plain version

After 1945 the Allies transferred the rights to the Free State of Bavaria, which used the copyright to block reprinting in Germany for 70 years. German copyright expired on 31 December 2015 (70 years after Hitler's death), so the German text has been public domain in Germany since 1 January 2016. That is what allowed the scholarly edition below. Copyright rules differ by country, and the translations have their own separate copyrights (see section 3).

2. Where to read the ORIGINAL GERMAN text

A. The scholarly edition — free online (best source)

Institut für Zeitgeschichte (IfZ), Munich — Hitler, Mein Kampf: Eine kritische Edition (2016) - Edited by Christian Hartmann, Thomas Vordermayer, Othmar Plöckinger, Roman Töppel and others for the Leibniz Institute for Contemporary History. - Print edition: 2 volumes, about 1,966 pages, roughly 3,500 explanatory notes, ISBN 9783981405231, priced €59. - Free online since July 2022: Mein Kampf critical edition (Institut für Zeitgeschichte) — verified open 2026-11-19. The full German text of both volumes, chapter by chapter, with the scholars' commentary alongside it. Search tips on the site: prefix u: searches only Hitler's original text; k: searches only the commentary. - Background page (English): ifz-muenchen.de - Note: there is no English translation of the critical edition — it is German only. The German text itself is the same book; the notes around it are the scholars' work.

3. English translations — what can actually be read free and legally

A. Murphy translation (1939) — free online ✔

  • Translator: Murphy. The unexpurgated translation, first published 21 March 1939 by Hurst & Blackett Ltd., London. Murphy died in 1946. This was the only English translation approved by Nazi Germany at the time.
  • Project Gutenberg Australia, full text, verified open 2026-11-19 (complete, ~50,800 lines): Project Gutenberg Australia
  • Project Gutenberg Australia states its eBooks are made from editions in the public domain in Australia and are free with almost no restrictions, with a warning to check your own country's law. The University of Pennsylvania's Online Books Page lists this same Murphy text as "text in Russia and Australia; NO US ACCESS" — i.e. its free status depends on jurisdiction. It is presented here exactly as those sources present it: a working, complete, free-to-read link, with that legal caveat stated openly.

B. Ralph Manheim translation (1943) — NOT legally free online

  • Translator: Ralph Manheim; published by Houghton Mifflin in 1943 and still published by them; the most commonly published English translation since the war (standard print edition ISBN 978-0395951057).
  • Its US copyright was renewed (© 29 Sep 1943; renewal R509011, 14 July 1971, Houghton Mifflin as proprietor) — so the Manheim translation is still in copyright in the US and is not legally free online. Copies floating around file-sharing sites are pirated and are not linked here. It can be bought in print or borrowed from a library.

C. Other period translations (for the record)

  • An abridged English edition appeared in 1933 (Hurst & Blackett / Houghton Mifflin) — shortened and softened; not recommended as "the book."
  • A 1939 Stackpole Sons (New York) unlicensed complete edition also exists from the wartime "translation war"; it is a period curiosity, not the standard text.

5. A short SAMPLE only — the book's first line

German (first sentence of Chapter 1):

"Als glückliche Bestimmung gilt es mir heute, daß das Schicksal mir zum Geburtsort gerade Braunau am Inn zuwies."

My translation of just that sentence:

"I count it a fortunate dispensation today that Fate assigned Braunau am Inn, of all places, to be my birthplace."

(For comparison, Murphy's published rendering is: "It has turned out fortunate for me to-day that destiny appointed Braunau-on-the-Inn to be my birthplace." Wording differs between translators; the German is the fixed original.)

6. About translating the whole book

The book is roughly 700+ pages. A full fresh translation is not reproduced here — instead, the plan is chapter-by-chapter translation on request: pick a chapter, and it will be translated from the German text in the IfZ edition (section 2A), with the Murphy translation (section 3A) available side-by-side for checking. That keeps each piece exact, sourced, and readable.
