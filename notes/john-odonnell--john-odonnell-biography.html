John O'Donnell — Biography

Researched and saved 2026-11-20. His other columns and controversies are in the companion file, odonnell-other-work.md, in this same folder.

John Parsons O'Donnell — Washington columnist for the New York Daily News, author of the daily column "Capitol Stuff."

Picture link:

The basic facts

  • Born: July 23, 1896, in Somerville, Massachusetts. Son of a physician (Wikipedia, citing his obituaries, calls the father "a wealthy physician").
  • Died: December 17, 1961, aged 65, at Georgetown University Hospital in Washington, D.C. Time magazine's obituary gives the cause: "of chronic congestive heart failure."
  • Education: Tufts College, B.A. 1920; graduate work at Harvard University and at Dijon University in France.
  • World War I: served as an infantry lieutenant. (The obituary-based accounts list his Tufts graduation in 1920 alongside the war service; the exact order — service before finishing his degree — is not spelled out in the free sources checked.)

Career

  • 1923–1927: Reporter and assistant city editor at the New York American, William Randolph Hearst's paper. Political journalism became his beat here.
  • 1927: Joined the New York Daily News. In his first years there he was a New York reporter — a Daily News photograph at Getty Images (see "Pictures" below) shows the young reporter O'Donnell interviewing the gangster Jack "Legs" Diamond.
  • 1933: Sent to Washington. He covered Franklin D. Roosevelt's 1933 presidential inauguration, became chief of the Daily News Washington bureau, and started the "Capitol Stuff" column. Time's obituary dates his bureau-chief tenure "1933–61." Historian Matthew Pressman writes that "John O'Donnell served as the political columnist from the early 1930s until his retirement in 1961" (Pressman 2021, cited below).
  • How he got the column: "Capitol Stuff" began as a husband-and-wife production. O'Donnell had married fellow Daily News reporter Doris Fleeson in 1930; she joined him in the Washington bureau in 1933, and, in the words of the University of Kansas archive that holds her papers, "Together they started the 'Capitol Stuff' column." The two had opposite politics — Fleeson was a fierce New Dealer, O'Donnell increasingly anti-Roosevelt — and after their divorce in 1942 O'Donnell wrote the column alone for the rest of his career.
  • 1939–1940: War correspondent for the Daily News in Europe; he spent time with British forces on the Maginot Line in France.
  • The column also ran in the Daily News's sister paper, the Washington Times-Herald — a 1947 U.S. Supreme Court petition in his libel case states that his bylined stories and "a column written by him under the heading 'Capitol Stuff' appeared regularly in the New York Daily News and the Washington Times Herald."
  • 1961: Retired, and died the same year.

Reputation and political leanings

O'Donnell was one of the most combative Washington columnists of his generation — a conservative, isolationist, anti-New Deal voice writing for the biggest-circulation newspaper in America.

  • Time magazine's obituary (Milestones, Dec. 29, 1961): "John Parsons O'Donnell, 65, longtime (1933-61) Washington bureau chief for the New York Daily News whose hard-hitting column, 'Capitol Stuff,' won him fame as one of his generation's top political reporters… An engaging Boston Irishman with limitless gusto for the mechanics of politics. O'Donnell larded his stories with strongly conservative and isolationist opinions that landed him in endless clamorous hassles (most notable: F.D.R.'s angry World War II press conference…" (the extract available to this research ends mid-sentence there).
  • Frank Holeman, who started as a copy boy and worked under O'Donnell in the Washington bureau from 1941, recalled in a 1991 National Press Club oral history: "the bureau chief was John O'Donnell, who was a very good man to me but had an awful lot of enemies around town, including FDR."
  • Encounter magazine (September 1990), in an obituary of a member of his family, described John O'Donnell as "the extreme spirit in Washington journalism of that notorious 'America-First isolationism' which campaigned against President Roosevelt's efforts to help the Allies against Hitler," and called him "an 'anti-anti-Nazi'."
  • Matthew Pressman's article (below) places O'Donnell's column inside the Daily News's broader editorial line of those years: populist, isolationist, anti-communist, and hostile to elites — the paper of Joseph Medill Patterson (publisher until his death in 1946) and the "McCormick-Patterson axis" of isolationist papers.

Personal life

  • First marriage: Kathryn Mullin (1902–1994), a stage performer billed as "The Girl With the Million Dollar Legs" and a noted women's saber-fencing champion. Married in a civil ceremony on May 6, 1927; divorced 1929.
  • Second marriage: Doris Fleeson, fellow Daily News reporter and his "Capitol Stuff" co-writer. Married September 28, 1930; their daughter, also named Doris, was born about two years later; divorced 1942, as their politics pulled apart.
  • The New York Times obituary (Dec. 18, 1961, p. 35) is reported to say he married and divorced three times. The third wife's name could not be verified in the free sources checked — the Times obituary itself is paywalled (citation below).

Cautions — errors in the easy sources

  • Wikipedia's article on O'Donnell contains mistakes; do not copy it blind. It says his Daily News-era writing "reflected the right-wing political biases of his publisher, William Randolph Hearst." Hearst was his publisher at the New York American (1923–1927). At the Daily News the publisher was Joseph Medill Patterson. Wikipedia also dates the Patton-column retraction to "Oct. 8, 1945"; Pressman's article and the contemporary Jewish press (The Sentinel, Oct. 25, 1945; The Jewish Post, Oct. 26, 1945) put the retraction column on October 19, 1945 — the date this project already has on file.
  • A different John O'Donnell: the "John O'Donnell Papers, 1945 (C0902)" at the State Historical Society of Missouri, described as a sports column written as letters to servicemen, belong to John Eugene O'Donnell (1902–1970), the Davenport, Iowa sportswriter — not to this man. Do not merge them.
  • No book by John Parsons O'Donnell turned up in the catalog searches run (Google Books / WorldCat-style searches). His life's work was the newspaper column; if he wrote a book, it left no trace in the sources checked.
