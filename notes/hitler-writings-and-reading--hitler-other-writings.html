Everything else Hitler wrote — catalogue with dates and where to read it

Researched 2026-11-19. One line per item, in date order. Links were returned by search/opened on that date; limits are flagged honestly. Two scholarly collections cover most of the early material:

  • Eberhard Jäckel & Axel Kuhn (eds.), Hitler: Sämtliche Aufzeichnungen 1905–1924 (Stuttgart: Deutsche Verlags-Anstalt, 1980) — over 600 documents: nearly everything Hitler wrote or said on record up to 1924, in German.
  • Institut für Zeitgeschichte (ed.), Hitler: Reden, Schriften, Anordnungen, Februar 1925 bis Januar 1933 (Munich, 1991–2003, 12 volumes) — the speeches, writings and orders from the party's refounding to the seizure of power, in German.

1. The Gemlich letter — 16 September 1919

His earliest known political writing. A letter to the soldier Adolf Gemlich, written at the order of Captain Karl Mayr of the Reichswehr's propaganda unit in Munich, answering a question about the army's position on the "Jewish question." It argues for a "rational" antisemitism — legal removal of Jewish rights rather than pogroms — ending with the goal of "the irrevocable removal of the Jews themselves." - The signed original is held by the Simon Wiesenthal Center (bought 2011; signature authenticated by Charles Hamilton in 1990). An unsigned typed copy sits in the Bavarian State Archives, Munich (HStA, Abt. IV, Gruppen Kdo. 4, Bd. 50/8). - Read the full English translation free: German History in Documents and Images (GHDI) — https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/ghdi:document-5110 (translation by Richard S. Levy; German text from Jäckel, Sämtliche Aufzeichnungen, pp. 88–90). Also at the Jewish Virtual Library: https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/adolf-hitler-s-first-anti-semitic-writing

2. The 25-Point Programme — proclaimed 24 February 1920

The founding programme of the party (still called the DAP that night; renamed NSDAP days later), read by Hitler to about 2,000 people in Munich's Hofbräuhaus. Not Hitler's alone: it was drafted jointly by Anton Drexler, Hitler, Gottfried Feder and Dietrich Eckart, adapting Rudolf Jung's Austro-Bohemian programme; the economic planks (e.g. "breaking of interest-slavery") are mainly Feder's. Hitler later declared the 25 points unchangeable. - Read the full English translation free: GHDI — https://ghdi.ghi-dc.org/pdf/eng/NAZI_GERMAN_WORKERS_ENG.pdf (translation from Noakes & Pridham, Nazism 1919–1945, Vol. 1).

3. "Why We Are Anti-Semites" speech — 13 August 1920

(Warum sind wir Antisemiten?) Hitler's first big set-piece antisemitic speech, delivered at a public NSDAP meeting in the great hall of the Munich Hofbräuhaus. Note on the date: most scholarship dates it 13 August 1920 (the text is in Jäckel, Sämtliche Aufzeichnungen, pp. 184–204, and was first published by Reginald Phelps in Vierteljahrshefte für Zeitgeschichte 16 (1968), pp. 390–420). Some websites print "15 August 1920" — that is an error that has been copied around; the scholarly edition is the safer date. - Where to read it: the full text exists in German in Jäckel and in the 1968 Vierteljahrshefte article. English renderings circulate on partisan websites translated from the 1968 journal text — usable for the wording but they are not scholarly editions; flagged honestly rather than linked as authorities.

4. Early articles in the Völkischer Beobachter — 1920–1923

After the party bought the Völkischer Beobachter in December 1920, Hitler wrote signed articles for it — e.g. "Rathenau und Sancho Pansa" ("Rathenau and Sancho Panza"), 13 March 1921. These pieces are collected, with his early speeches and notes, in Jäckel's Sämtliche Aufzeichnungen 1905–1924 (above). The newspaper itself survives on microfilm at the Library of Congress (Newspaper & Current Periodical Reading Room) and the US Holocaust Memorial Museum library — it is printed in Fraktur and is not freely readable online as a complete run; flagged as a research-room source, not a link.

5. Mein Kampf, Volumes 1–2 — 1925 / 1926

His main book. Full details, the original German text and the readable English translation are in the companion file: mein-kampf-original-and-translation.md.

6. The "Second Book" (Zweites Buch) — written 1928, published 1961

A book-length manuscript on foreign policy (Lebensraum in the East, alliance policy), dictated in June–July 1928 after the party's disappointing Reichstag election. Hitler never published it — the Eher Verlag shelved it. The manuscript ended up in captured German records in Alexandria, Virginia, where historian Gerhard L. Weinberg found it in 1958. The Institut für Zeitgeschichte published it in 1961 as Hitlers Zweites Buch: Ein Dokument aus dem Jahr 1928 (Stuttgart), edited by Weinberg. A revised, better-annotated version appeared inside the IfZ's Reden, Schriften, Anordnungen edition (1995). - Read it: English translation by Krista Smith, ed. Weinberg, Hitler's Second Book: The Unpublished Sequel to Mein Kampf (Enigma Books, 2003/2006, ISBN 978-1-929631-61-2). A full text of that translation is on the Internet Archive: https://archive.org/stream/hitlers-second-book/Hitler%27s%20Second%20Book_djvu.txt — flagged: the German original is public domain, but this 2003 English translation is a commercial edition; the Archive copy's legal status is the Archive's responsibility, noted honestly.

7. The speeches as a body — Domarus (and the IfZ edition)

  • Max Domarus (ed.), Hitler: Speeches and Proclamations 1932–1945: The Chronicle of a Dictatorship — about 3,400 pages. German original: Hitler: Reden und Proklamationen 1932–1945 (1962–63). English edition (Bolchazy-Carducci), translated by Mary Fran Gilbert and Chris Wilcox, 4 volumes: Vol. 1, 1932–1934 (1990, ISBN 0-86516-227-1); Vol. 2, 1935–1938 (1992, ISBN 0-86516-229-8); Vol. 3, 1939–1940 (1997, ISBN 0-86516-230-1); Vol. 4, 1941–1945 (2004, ISBN 0-86516-231-X). Generally regarded as the standard day-by-day record of his public statements — a library/purchase set, not free online.
  • For 1925–1933 speeches, the scholarly source is the IfZ Reden, Schriften, Anordnungen (see headnote). Individual famous speeches (e.g. the 1939 Reichstag "prophecy" speech) also circulate in translation through GHDI and the US Holocaust Memorial Museum.

8. Table Talk (Tischgespräche / Monologe) — recorded 1941–1944 ⚠ handle with care

These are not writings by Hitler's own hand. They are mealtime monologues at his military headquarters, written down by aides (Heinrich Heim, then Henry Picker) working for Martin Bormann, who reviewed and edited the notes. Published versions: Picker's German edition (1951); the French edition by François Genoud (1952); the English Hitler's Table Talk (1953, trans. Cameron & Stevens, intro. Hugh Trevor-Roper); the German Monologe im Führerhauptquartier (ed. Werner Jochmann, 1980). - Authenticity/editorial cautions (the reason for the flag): the original notebooks are lost; Bormann edited the notes to suit his own views; Genoud's French version — from which the English was retranslated — is documented to contain insertions and distortions (historian Mikael Nilsson's research), and Genoud separately forged a so-called "Hitler's Political Testament" (a different document from the real April 1945 testament below — do not confuse them). Mainstream historians still use Table Talk, but only cautiously and preferably in the German Jochmann/Picker texts, never as word-for-word stenography. Any single striking quote from the English Table Talk should be checked against the German before being trusted.

9. Last will and political testament — 29 April 1945

Dictated in the Berlin bunker in the early hours of 29 April 1945 (signed 4:00 a.m.), the day before his suicide. Two documents: - Private will: marriage to Eva Braun, disposal of his property, Bormann named executor, request that their bodies be burned. Witnesses: Goebbels, Bormann, von Below. - Political testament (two parts): a final justification of his life and the war; then the succession — Göring and Himmler expelled, Dönitz named Reich President, Goebbels Chancellor, Bormann Party Minister — closing with an instruction to uphold the race laws and resist "international Jewry." Witnesses: Goebbels, Burgdorf, Bormann, Krebs. - Read both in full, from the Nuremberg record (Nazi Conspiracy and Aggression, Doc. 3569-PS): http://www.ibiblio.org/pha/policy/1945/450429a.html — verified source text. The German original is printed in the IMT "blue series," Vol. 41, pp. 547–554. - ⚠ Not this document: the "Hitler's Last Testament" / bunker conversations published by Trevor-Roper from Genoud's papers are regarded as Genoud fabrications (see item 8).
