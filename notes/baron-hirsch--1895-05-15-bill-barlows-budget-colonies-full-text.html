BARON HIRSCH'S COLONIES.

Those in South America May Now Apparently Be Regarded Successful.

"The report for 1894 of the Jewish Colonization Association shows that the Jewish colonies in the Argentine are gradually but surely passing the experimental stage. Baron Hirsch is no dreamer of dreams. He knows the limits of the power of his wealth, and he does not believe in modern miracles. The company has had to repair blunders of administration on both sides of the Atlantic, but they were not irreparable, and, according to the New York Evening Post, the experience, though dearly bought, has not been profitless. Each colonist will become the absolute owner of his land by the annual payment of $100, or a little over a dollar per acre. A medical staff, schools and flour mills are being established and the cost of administration is being diminished. Before this year four colonies were established, having a total area of 200,000 acres, occupied by 563 families.

"Ten groups, comprising an average of about forty families, about 400 families in all, were settled in the Argentine in the year 1894. Horses, cattle and agricultural tools were provided in good time and the newcomers found everything prepared on their arrival. Nearly every family has received an allotment of 185 acres. The central administration is occupied with the formation of new groups which can be forwarded next year. Measures are being taken, both in Russia and in the Argentine, for the arrangement of these groups, so that from 200 to 300 families may leave successively in batches from May, 1895. The association owns 444,780 acres, of which 240,097 are unoccupied, and the central administration contemplates the purchase of considerable further tracts in the course of this year. Owing to the excellent arrangements of the committees in Russia, more than 3,000 persons were forwarded in the space of a few months without undue delay or difficulty. Since its foundation up to Sept. 30 last the association has spent $1,851,545, of which no less than $900,000 has been written off as lost. The loss is notable as a proof that by way of mere mercantile speculation the project could not have repaid its founders, though the gain to humanity may well exceed the large sum which Baron Hirsch has been content to devote to his noble object. The interest received from interim investments of the funds placed at the disposal of the association amounts to about $300,000 and the produce in wheat actually grown is valued at about the same amount."

Notes

  • The four colonies are NOT named in this article — it says only "four colonies were established." No Argentine locations or individual colony sizes are given.
  • Key figures: 4 colonies, 200,000 acres, 563 families; ~400 more families settled in 1894; allotments of 185 acres per family; land ownership by annual payment of $100 ("a little over a dollar per acre"); the association owned 444,780 acres total (240,097 unoccupied); spent $1,851,545 by Sept. 30, 1894, of which $900,000 written off as lost; wheat grown valued at about $300,000; 200–300 more families to leave in batches from May 1895.
