Hirsch's will and what happened after — newspaper sweep

James's ask (2026-11-19): find everything on Hirsch, from his beginning till end — including the early years, the will, and the aftermath. The life arc itself is in hirsch-beginning-to-end.md. This file is the newspaper sweep of the will and the aftermath.

The will: Clara sole legatee — and a surprise heiress story

Democratic Sentinel (Indiana), July 10, 1896 — "RICHEST HEIRESS ON EARTH" (https://newspapers.library.in.gov/?a=d&d=DMST18960710.2.41 — reprinting the New York Journal):

  • Lucienne Premelle Hirsch, 14 years old, granddaughter of the late Baron Maurice de Hirsch, "will eventually inherit" a $130,000,000 fortune — "the greatest heiress in the world."
  • Her mother "was a governess in Baron Hirsch's family, and her father was the Baron's only son" — Lucien, who died in 1887. She lives in Brussels, "being reared a strict Catholic," under her guardian and governess Mme. Untime Montefiore Levy, née Bischoffsheim, sister of the Baroness de Hirsch.
  • "Though the Baroness de Hirsch is named in her late husband's testament as his sole legatee, it is stated upon unquestionably authority that, acting under private instructions, which the Baron gave her not many months before his death, she will hold this vast wealth in trust until Lucienne comes of age, when she will turn it over to her without reserve."
  • The will also set apart $200,000 for Lucienne's maintenance until she reaches her majority, half the income going to her guardian, who "will not be called upon to render any account of the same."

Handle with care: this is a sensational newspaper story, sourced to the New York Journal, and it sits uneasily next to the standard biographies, which say Lucien died unmarried and childless in 1887 and that Hirsch's heir was "humanity." It may be gossip, a garbled version of the adopted sons' story (see Clara's will below), or a real secret granddaughter. It is saved here as what the papers printed in July 1896, not as a settled fact — worth checking against the Hirsch family history (Prys, Die Familie von Hirsch auf Gereuth, 1931) before treating it as true.

Clara's will (died April 1, 1899) — the bequests in the papers

An Australian paper's report on the probate of Baroness Clara de Hirsch's will (Trove, National Library of Australia: https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/rendition/nla.news-page13409226.pdf — OCR is rough, figures below are the clear ones):

  • Her will bore date September 23, 1896, with 15 codicils, the last dated August 15, 1898. Her estate in England was assessed at £51,277 3s. She directed that, being an Austrian subject, her estate be wound up and administered in Vienna.
  • Chief charitable bequests (francs unless noted): Jewish Colonization Association, London — 10,000,000f; Baron de Hirsch Fund, New York — $1,200,000; Baron de Hirsch Institute, Montreal — $1,200,000; Baron de Hirsch Foundation for schools in Galicia — 2,200,000f (per the Wikipedia bequest list); Œuvre de nourriture (food and clothing for poor children in Alliance schools) — $600,000; Pasteur Institute and Philanthropic Society of Paris — $500,000 each; a $700,000 pension fund for the officials of her husband's Oriental railways; $200,000 to build the Clara de Hirsch Home for Working Girls, endowed with $600,000 more; $150,000 for the Baron de Hirsch Trade School building in New York.
  • Family provision: $4,800,000 for her two adopted sons, Maurice and Raymond de Forest (the Wikipedia bequest list; the Trove report's OCR is too garbled to read the franc figures for the family bequests reliably, and it also mentions a bequest to an adopted daughter); $1,000,000 each to her brothers-in-law Emil and Theodor von Hirsch.
  • The executors named included Jules Dietz, 3 Rue des Mathurins, Paris.
  • The totals line up with the life arc: she gave away more than $15,000,000 in her three years of widowhood and left a further $10,000,000 in her will, intending to give away essentially the whole fortune.

The JCA after them

  • With funding of more than $36 million, the Jewish Colonization Association was probably the largest charitable trust in the world in its day (Jewish Currents, "April 21: Baron de Hirsch": https://jewishcurrents.org/april-21-baron-de-hirsch). It also set up Jewish farm settlements in New Jersey, Connecticut, Canada, and elsewhere, plus technical schools, cooperative factories, and savings and loan banks.
  • Clara's death raised the JCA's capital to £11,000,000.

Early years — still thin in the newspapers

The newspaper sweep found plenty on Hirsch's death (April 1896) and on Clara's will (1899), but almost nothing in the American papers on his early years in Munich and Brussels (1831–1869) beyond what the biographies give — born December 9, 1831, Brussels schooling at 13, Bischoffsheim & Goldschmidt at 17, married Clara June 28, 1855, the 1869 Ottoman railway concession. If James wants, the next hunt is European papers for the 1869 concession fight and the 1890 sale to Deutsche Bank.
