Coughlin's Social Justice — "Imported" Communists / Communism: Full-Search Results

Search date: November 19, 2026 Paper: Social Justice, Father Charles Coughlin's national weekly, Royal Oak, Michigan (1936–1942), as hosted on archive.org in the "Padre Charles Coughlin" collection (issue text files named socnew_*_text.pdf).

What was searched for: every passage where the paper uses "import" / "imported" / "importation" in connection with communists, communism, Russia, the C.I.O., Trotsky, or Jews — i.e., claims that communists or communism were imported into Russia, or imported from Russia into America or elsewhere.

Method: Fetched the full OCR text of individual issues from archive.org and searched each issue's complete text for every occurrence of "import", then read each occurrence in context. Also ran targeted web searches (e.g., site:archive.org socnew "imported" Coughlin; "imported communism" OR "imported Moscow" Coughlin "Social Justice"), which surfaced only the two already-known items below.

Coverage — read this before treating this as complete: The full run is roughly 300 weekly issues. The issues below were grepped in full (every "import" occurrence checked in context) — 12 issues spanning all seven years of the run:

  • April 24, 1936 (Vol. I, No. 7)
  • March 22, 1937 (Vol. III, No. 12)
  • June 14, 1937 (Vol. III, No. 24)
  • May 16, 1938 (Vol. 1A, No. 11)
  • June 13, 1938 (Vol. 1A, No. 15)
  • February 6, 1939 (Vol. 3A, No. 6)
  • April 24, 1939 (Vol. 3A, No. 17)
  • September 11, 1939 (Vol. 4A, No. 11)
  • January 1, 1940 (Vol. 5A, No. 1) — known item; text via the unz.org scan in the archive.org "Social Justice 1936–1942" item
  • January 20, 1941
  • July 7, 1941
  • January 12, 1942 (Vol. 9, No. 2)

This is NOT an exhaustive search of all ~300 issues. Issues not listed above were not individually grepped. Results below are everything found in the covered issues plus the two previously known finds.

Already found (starting points from the earlier first pass)

1. June 13, 1938 — Vol. 1A, No. 15

Front-page article on communism ("Communism — The Theory" issue).

"In 1917, the international and imported troublemakers from America and Hungary — Trotzky and Bela Kun, to be specific — went to Russia."

Note the direction: the troublemakers are imported into Russia from America and Hungary. The passage goes on: "Internationalists poured into Spain nine years ago… They reechoed the promises made by Trotzky and Lenin and Bela Kun to the Spaniards."

Full-issue check (Nov. 19, 2026): this is the only on-theme use of "import" in this issue. The issue's other "import" hits are "important/importance" and a trade passage about "importing tremendously large amounts of agricultural commodities from other countries."

URL: https://archive.org/download/socnew_1938a_vol01a_11_202602/socnew_1938a_vol01a_15_text.pdf

2. January 1, 1940 — Vol. 5A, No. 1

Front-page editorial (headline OCR-garbled, apparently "Editor's 1940 Challenge — Must Halt Excesses of Red Leaders") [OCR rough].

"The C.I.O., under the inspiration of Communist leadership, has provoked labor warfare. It imported the sit-down strike from Russia, and used it to [use it as a Moscow instrument — column text interleaved in OCR]."

URL (unz.org scan in the archive.org "Social Justice 1936–1942" item): https://dn721809.ca.archive.org/0/items/social-justice-1936-1942/Social%20Justice/1940/Social%20Justice%20v5An01%20%281940-01-01%29%20%28unz.org%29.pdf

NEW FINDS

NEW 1 — September 11, 1939 — Vol. 4A, No. 11

"The News" column, item headline: "He Rescued Bolivia, Ousted the Financiers, Then Shot Himself?"

"In 1936, these internationalists set up Col. David Toro as president to head a socialist state under imported Moscow Communism. In July, 1937, a bloodless coup led by the 33-year-old Lieut. Col. Busch threw the Left out."

This is the clearest additional example of the exact theme: communism described as imported from Moscow into another country (Bolivia).

URL: https://archive.org/download/socnew_1939_vol04a_01/socnew_1939_vol04a_11_text.pdf

NEW 2 — March 22, 1937 — Vol. III, No. 12

Front-page article, headline: "Red Plotters Push Drive in Coal Regions — Own Agitators Disclose How Leaders Are Carrying Out Orders From Moscow"

"For years the anthracite coal operators imported thousands of poor immigrants from the eastern countries of Europe as cheap labor, and created this exploitation [which] left the area an easy prey to the penetrating poison which emanates from Moscow."

(The bracketed "[which]" is an editorial bridge — the OCR column layout drops the connective word. The rest of the sentence is clean.)

Context: the article says Communist agitators in the Pennsylvania anthracite fields (it names organizer Steve Nelson) exploited the coal operators' imported cheap labor to organize the unemployed of the Wilkes-Barre area "into Communism," taking their orders from Moscow. So the claim runs: operators imported the immigrants; Moscow's poison then worked on them.

URL: https://archive.org/download/socnew_1938a_vol01a_11_202602/socnew_1937_vol03_12_text.pdf

BORDERLINE / RELATED FINDS (same word, looser connection — included so nothing is hidden)

B1 — March 22, 1937 — Vol. III, No. 12 (same issue as NEW 2)

Editorial page, in the editorial attack on the proposed child-labor amendment (which the paper calls a "youth regimentation amendment") and New Deal measures. Surviving fragment:

"…imported from dictatorial lands, would be within their rights…" [OCR rough]

The OCR of this page interleaves three columns, so the full sentence cannot be cleanly recovered from the text file. What is clear: a New Deal scheme is being attacked as something "imported from dictatorial lands." "Dictatorial lands" is not named as Russia/communism in the recoverable fragment, so this is borderline.

URL: https://archive.org/download/socnew_1938a_vol01a_11_202602/socnew_1937_vol03_12_text.pdf

B2 — April 24, 1939 — Vol. 3A, No. 17

Front-page editorial, headline: "President Roosevelt Wants War"

"Why are aliens imported into America by the boatload, while American citizens are literally kicked out of their jobs in favor of foreigners?"

This is about "aliens"/foreigners generally — communists are not named in the sentence — so it is borderline for this theme, but it is the same "imported" framing the paper uses elsewhere for radicals.

URL: https://archive.org/download/socnew_1938a_vol01a_11_202602/socnew_1939_vol03a_17_text.pdf

Issues grepped in full with NO on-theme use of "import"

  • April 24, 1936 (Vol. I, No. 7): only farm-product imports.
  • June 14, 1937 (Vol. III, No. 24): only trade imports.
  • May 16, 1938 (Vol. 1A, No. 11): only "important/importance," "boycott Japanese imports," and a tariff item on "imported crockery."
  • February 6, 1939 (Vol. 3A, No. 6): no on-theme use.
  • January 20, 1941: no on-theme use.
  • July 7, 1941: only trade ("importing from South America," "export-import dollar figures"). (The issue does discuss who gets "credit for inventing the sitdown [strike]," but without the word "import.")
  • January 12, 1942 (Vol. 9, No. 2): no on-theme use.

Bottom line

Beyond the two already-known passages (June 13, 1938 and January 1, 1940), the covered issues produced 2 clear new finds (September 11, 1939 — "imported Moscow Communism" in Bolivia; March 22, 1937 — coal operators "imported" the immigrants Moscow's agitators then worked on) and 2 borderline finds (March 22, 1937 — "imported from dictatorial lands"; April 24, 1939 — "aliens imported into America by the boatload"). The remaining ~290 issues of the run were not individually grepped, so more examples may exist in uncovered issues.
