The Korherr Report (March 23, 1943)

What it is: a statistical report titled The Final Solution of the European Jewish Question, written by Dr. Richard Korherr, the SS's Inspector for Statistics, at Himmler's request, covering the period up to December 31, 1942. It is stamped "Secret Reich Matter." The original is held by the US National Archives (Record Group 238, document NO-5194) — it was a Nuremberg prosecution document. Korherr was not a propagandist writing for the public; this was an internal accounting document for the head of the SS.

Read the document itself (full English translation, German History in Documents and Images): https://germanhistorydocs.org/en/nazi-germany-1933-1945/statistical-report-on-the-final-solution-known-as-the-korherr-report-march-23-1943

What it records (Section V, "The Evacuation of the Jews")

All evacuations from October 1939 to December 31, 1942, per the Reich Security Main Office's tabulations:

Korherr's line item Number
Evacuation of Jews from Baden and the Palatinate to France 6,504
Evacuation from Reich territory incl. the Protectorate and Bialystok district, to the East 170,642
Evacuation from Reich territory and the Protectorate to Theresienstadt 87,193
Transportation of Jews from the eastern provinces to the Russian East 1,449,692
— of whom "transited through the camps in the General Government" 1,274,166
— "through the camps in the Warthegau" 145,301
Evacuations from other countries (France 41,911; Netherlands 38,571; Belgium 16,886; Norway 532; Slovakia 56,691; Croatia 4,927) —
"Evacuation overall (incl. Theresienstadt and incl. special treatment)" 1,873,549
Added separately, per the Reich Security Main Office: evacuation of Jews "in the Russian territories including the former Baltic countries since the beginning of the eastern campaign" 633,300

The sentence that matters most (Section X, "Balance Sheet for the Jews in Europe")

Korherr's own summary, in the report's words:

"Between 1937 and the beginning of 1943, the number of Jews in Europe is likely to have declined by an estimated 4 million, in part through emigration, in part through excess mortality of the Jews in central and western Europe, in part through the evacuations especially in the eastern territories…"

And:

"…it is likely that since 1933… European Jewry is likely to have lost close to half of its population."

That is the SS's own statistician, writing for Himmler in March 1943 — with the war's deadliest camp year (1943) and the Hungarian deportations of 1944 still ahead of him — already recording a decline of about four million and the loss of close to half of European Jewry.

The euphemisms, flagged

  • "Evacuation" (Evakuierung) — deportation, overwhelmingly to killing centers or shooting sites. The 1,274,166 who "transited through the camps in the General Government" are the Operation Reinhard camp victims; the camps (Bełżec, Sobibór, Treblinka) had no capacity to hold or forward anything like that number of people, and almost none of them appear alive anywhere afterward.
  • "Special treatment" (Sonderbehandlung) — killing. Korherr's total line includes it in so many words: "incl. special treatment."
  • "Transited / sifted through" (durchgeschleust) — passed through camps from which there was no onward transport record.

Why it undercounts (Korherr says so himself)

  • The evacuation table states: "The above numbers do not include the inmates of the ghettos and concentration camps."
  • On the Soviet territories he admits: "only a part of the deaths of Soviet Russian Jews in the occupied eastern territories are recorded here, while those in the rest of European Russia and on the front are not included at all."
  • It stops at December 31, 1942 — before Treblinka and Sobibór finished operating, before Auschwitz's peak year, and before the ~430,000 Hungarian Jews deported in 1944.

Cross-check

Korherr's 1,274,166 for the General Government camps matches, to the digit, the total in the Höfle Telegram — an entirely separate SS document (a radio message the British intercepted). See hofle-telegram.md. Two independent pieces of the regime's own paperwork agreeing exactly is what a real count looks like.
