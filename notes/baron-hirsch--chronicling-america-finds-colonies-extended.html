Chronicling America finds — the Argentine colonies, extended search (completed 2026-11-19)

Extended loc.gov pass, 1891–1900, for the colonies by name (Moiseville, Mauricio, Clara, Lucienville) and by topic. Six pages verified in the "Image w/Text" view. The May 15, 1895 Bill Barlow's Budget colonies report is in its own file and is not counted again here.

1. Moiseville — the failure report. New-York Tribune, August 7, 1892, Image 1 of 24 (front page) Headline: "BARON HIRSCH'S COLONY A FAILURE." Quote: "London, Aug. 6.—A letter printed in yesterday's issue of 'The Jewish Chronicle' confirms the failure of Baron de Hirsch's colony in the Argentine Republic. The writer declares that the condition of affairs at Moiseville (the name of the colony) baffles description. The land selected for the settlement was ill chosen, and an enormous number of families are huddled together in tents and sheds, where they have been living for months in idleness and intrigue." Also: "He was obliged to ask for police protection, and broke up the colony. Eight hundred of the colonists have sailed for Europe within a month." (Colonel Goldsmid's attempt to reform the colony is described in the same item. Ten other August 1892 papers carried the same dispatch.) https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030214/1892-08-07/ed-1/?sp=1&q=moiseville

2. Moiseville — still receiving emigrants in 1900. The Pacific Commercial Advertiser (Honolulu), August 4, 1900, Image 10 of 16 Short news paragraph, no headline. Quote: "Jewish emigrants from Russia to South America are now able to travel to their destination without having to go to Antwerp. During the last month a batch of 363 emigrants arrived at Havre on their way to Moiseville Colony in the Argentine. Their impedimenta weighed over seventy tons." https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85047084/1900-08-04/ed-1/?sp=10&q=moiseville

3. Hirsch interview on the colonies. People's Voice (Wellington, Kan.), September 23, 1892, Image 2 of 8 Headline: "Jewish Colonies In Argentine." Quote: "In a recent interview Baron de Hirsch spoke of the new Jewish colonies in the Argentine Republic He said he had sent 6,000 Jews there and had negotiated for the purchase of 7,000,000 acres of land, but the government would grant only 5,000,000, as it did not wish too many aliens to settle in a body." Also: "There are now three Jewish colonies in the province of Buenos Ayres one with 80,000 acres, one with 40,000 and one with 20,000." The colonies are not named. The settlers "quickly became accustomed to their new profession, and to-day they are as hard working and capable farmers as emigrants belonging to any other religion"; 15,000 to 20,000 more were to be sent that year. https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn85032801/1892-09-23/ed-1/?sp=2&q=jewish+colonies+argentine

4. 1897 report. New York Journal and Advertiser, July 31, 1897, Image 11 of 14 In the "Among the Israelites" briefs. Quote: "More encouraging reports have lately been received from the Jewish colonies in Argentina." Same section, on Woodbine: "The Baron de Hirsch colony of Woodbine is thriving… There are now 1,200 colonists at Woodbine, an increase of about 500 within a year." https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn83030180/1897-07-31/ed-1/?sp=11&q=jewish+colonies+argentine

5. The 1895 colonies, corroborating the report. The Daily Morning Journal and Courier (New Haven, Conn.), October 1, 1895, Image 4 of 8 Short paragraph, no headline. Quote: "In Baron Hirsch's Argentine colonies for poor Hebrews each settler receives about 185 acres of land on payment of $165, and four mills, schools and a medical staff have been provided for nearly 1,000 families comprising the colonies, of which about 400 families located last year." ("Four mills" as printed; evidently flour mills, as in the May 1895 report.) https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn84020358/1895-10-01/ed-1/?sp=4&q=hirsch+colonies

6. The settlers' memorial to Hirsch. Narragansett Herald (Narragansett Pier, R.I.), January 23, 1897, Image 5 of 8 Short paragraph, no headline. Quote: "Baron Hirsch has secured at least one monument 'more lasting than bronze.' With the avowed object of commemorating the great benefits which the late baron conferred on the settlers in his Argentine colonies the 3 heads of families there have decided to give the name of Moses Hirsch to every male child until the first anniversary of his death." ("The 3 heads of families" as the scan reads; the sense is the heads of families.) https://www.loc.gov/resource/sn92063989/1897-01-23/ed-1/?sp=5&q=clara+hirsch+argentine

What came up empty, stated plainly

  • No American newspaper page naming Colonia Mauricio or Lucienville was found. "Lucienville" and "Colonia Mauricio" returned no results at all; "Mauricio" with "Hirsch" returned 3 pages, none about the colony.
  • No page naming the Clara colony was found. The one "Colonia Clara" hit was two ship names in a Klondike story; "Clara" + "Hirsch" + "Argentine" returned 95 results, almost all about the Baroness, not the colony.
  • "Moisesville" (with the second s) returned no results; the papers spelled it "Moiseville."
  • Moiseville is the only one of the four colonies the U.S. papers named in this pass. Note the loc.gov searches repeatedly showed "Your search timed out — some results may not be displayed," so an empty result means "not found through tthe search," not proof nothing exists.
