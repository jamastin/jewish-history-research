Epstein — The Released Documents

The Epstein documents as they have actually been released — the Justice Department's Epstein Library, the House Oversight Committee releases, and the unsealed court files: what each set is, where it lives, and what the Rothschild names in them actually show.

Jeffrey Epstein's mugshot, July 8, 2019 (New York State Sex Offender Registry photo). Source: Wikimedia Commons

3 document sets · DOJ Epstein Library · House Oversight releases · Court documents

Part One — The DOJ Epstein Library (the big one)

Under the Epstein Files Transparency Act (signed November 19, 2025), the Department of Justice collected roughly six million pages and released about half of them: a first release on December 19, 2025, and the largest on January 30, 2026 — about 3.5 million pages in all, with 2,000 videos and 180,000 images. The release is organized in Data Sets 1–12, and every page carries an EFTA number, which is how the pages are cited on this page.

One caution about the release itself: the January 2026 release had redaction failures that exposed survivor information, and the Department pulled roughly 47,000 files back; about 2.7 million pages remained up.

Part Two — House Oversight Committee releases

The House Oversight Committee released 33,295 pages the Justice Department produced to it on September 2, 2025. Estate material followed: the 2003 birthday book and further estate papers on September 8, 2025, and 20,000 or more additional estate pages on November 12, 2025. By November 2025 the committee's releases totalled roughly 65,000 pages. These carry DOJ-OGR and HOUSE_OVERSIGHT numbers instead of EFTA numbers.

Part Three — The court documents

In January 2024 the Southern District of New York unsealed the filings in Giuffre v. Maxwell (Case 1:15-cv-07433), entered as ECF No. 1320 and filed January 3, 2024 — about 943 pages of depositions, declarations, exhibits, and flight records that had been sealed or redacted for years.

The Rothschild mentions — what the documents show

By the counts in the released files, Ariane de Rothschild's name appears over 4,000 times and the bank over 1,600 times; one research count puts "Rothschild" at 10,887 documents in the DOJ release. The documented relationship runs from 2013 to April 2019, and the documents show a business and advisory relationship.

  • EFTA00310331 — Draft agreement, September 10, 2015, between Epstein's Southern Trust Company and Edmond de Rothschild Holding S.A., with Ariane de Rothschild signing off. The fee was tied to the bank's own settlement with the Justice Department: $25 million if the penalty came in under $75 million; $10 million if between $75 and $150 million. The bank settled on December 18, 2015 for $45.245 million; Epstein collected the $25 million.
  • EFTA01285900 — A $10,000,000 wire from Edmond de Rothschild (Suisse) SA, Geneva, December 17, 2015, and a second transfer of $14,999,980 from Benjamin de Rothschild, December 21, 2015 — together, the $25 million fee.
  • EFTA01119622 — A Southern Trust Company invoice, December 15, 2015, addressed to Ariane de Rothschild for $15 million, with Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas wire instructions. An invoice is not proof the money moved; it is recorded here as an invoice.
  • EFTA02456252 — Email exchange, July 20, 2016: Epstein sent Ariane de Rothschild a New York Times link on the 1MDB scandal and warned her about US prosecutors; her reply, sent from a Luxembourg board meeting, is quoted above.
  • EFTA02470755 — Epstein to Peter Thiel, 2016: "As you probably know, I represent the Rothschilds."
  • EFTA02498195 — Epstein to Larry Summers, July 6, 2015: "rothschild will be with me 20 21 in new york." The page in the Epstein Index
  • EFTA00690583 — A Richard Kahn memo on Paris antique furniture Epstein bought for the Rothschild household: $64,506 plus €86,896 delivered; €312,616 bought but not yet delivered.
  • EFTA01110892 and EFTA00648735 — Draft or signed agreements with Edmond de Rothschild entities and associated persons.
  • EFTA00080250 — Transaction-chart material including Edmond de Rothschild payments, and the SDNY prosecution exhibit headed "EXHIBIT A: LEON BLACK / ROTHSCHILD GROUP TRANSACTIONS" — 21 wires, $148 million in total.
  • House Oversight estate emails, released November 12, 2025 — Epstein planned to see a Broadway play with de Rothschild in January 2014 and a private trip with her to Montreal in September 2014 (reported from the release by Drop Site News, November 18, 2025).
  • Leon Black transcribed interview, June 26, 2026 — Black, explaining why he paid Epstein, listed Epstein's network of relationships, naming Ariane de Rothschild among them. The full transcript is linked in Part Two above.

The name in the old newspapers

The name Epstein is much older than Jeffrey Epstein. A full-text search of Chronicling America, the Library of Congress newspaper archive, returns 37,760 pages mentioning an Epstein of some kind. The oldest is Der liberale Beobachter of Reading, Pennsylvania, May 12, 1840. The oldest 100 matching pages, running from 1840 to 1854, are saved in the research files for this section.

The oldest match — Der liberale Beobachter, May 12, 1840, page 4 (Library of Congress)

Read with care

Being named in these documents — in an email, a flight log, or a deposition — is not by itself evidence of wrongdoing. On the Rothschild material specifically: no reviewed document shows Ariane de Rothschild took part in Epstein's sexual crimes. What the documents show is a business and advisory relationship, set out above with its page numbers so it can be checked.
