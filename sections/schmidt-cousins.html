Hitler's Schmidt Cousins — Arrested in Austria, 1945

Five of Hitler's Austrian relatives were arrested by Soviet forces in May 1945. Three of them — and a fourth by marriage — died in Soviet custody; one was released in 1955; Russia pardoned them all in 1997.

Austria and Moscow, 1945–1997 · A photograph of the cousins is being sourced; none is posted rather than post the wrong picture

Who they were

The Schmidts were Hitler's first cousins, from the Waldviertel in Austria — his mother's side of the family. In May 1945, as Soviet forces occupied the region, five of them were arrested: the first cousins Maria, Johann, and Eduard Schmidt, Maria's husband Ignaz Koppensteiner, and Johann Schmidt Jr.

What happened to them

Koppensteiner was arrested on the stated ground that he "approved of [Hitler's] criminal plans against the USSR." He died in a Moscow prison in 1949. Eduard Schmidt died in Soviet custody in 1951, and Maria Schmidt in 1953. Johann Schmidt Jr. was released in 1955. In 1997, Russia posthumously pardoned them all. The checked source in hand does not state Johann Schmidt Sr.'s individual outcome; that detail is left open rather than guessed.

A correction, plainly

A widely shared narrated video about Hitler's relatives says Johann Schmidt was taken to Vienna and "died in custody." That compresses and garbles the record above — the arrests were in Austria, the deaths in Soviet custody in Moscow, across 1949–1953. This story is also not to be confused with a different cousin, Aloisia Veit, who was murdered in Austria in 1940 — not by the Soviets and not by Jews, but by the Nazi regime's own euthanasia programme at Hartheim. That checked story is told in full in the research notes: The "Jews killed Hitler's cousin" story — checked.
