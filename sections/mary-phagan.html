Mary Phagan

The fourteen-year-old Atlanta girl whose murder on April 26, 1913 set off the Leo Frank case.

Mary Phagan, in a photograph printed by the Atlanta Journal. Source: Wikimedia Commons

The murder

Mary Phagan was murdered on April 26, 1913. Leo Frank, the superintendent of Atlanta's National Pencil Factory, was tried for the crime, and on August 25, 1913 the jury found him guilty — the Washington Herald's front page the next morning read: "found guilty of the murder of fourteen-year-old Mary Phagan on April 26."

Her name in the case that followed

When Governor Slaton commuted Frank's death sentence in 1915, the men who took Frank from the state prison farm and lynched him that August said they acted in her memory. Their own account says the plan was to hang him at Mary Phagan's cemetery; daybreak overtook them, and it was done instead in a grove near her birthplace, where she had played as a girl. The same account called her "whom all Cobb county loved." Her name runs through the whole newspaper record of the case: a search of the Library of Congress newspaper archive for "Mary Phagan" returns 1,896 pages for 1913–1916.
