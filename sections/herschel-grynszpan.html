Herschel Grynszpan

The Polish-Jewish teenager whose shots in Paris on November 7, 1938 were used as the pretext for Kristallnacht.

Herschel Grynszpan after his arrest in Paris, November 1938 (Bundesarchiv Bild 146-1988-078-07). Source: Wikimedia Commons

1921 – fate after 1942 unknown

His family, and the expulsion

Herschel Grynszpan was born in 1921 in Hanover, Germany, to Polish-Jewish parents, and was living in Paris. In late October 1938, Germany expelled some 17,000 Polish Jews in the action known as the Polenaktion; his parents were among them, put across the Polish border and stranded at Zbąszyń. Word reached him in Paris — a postcard from his sister described the family's plight.

The shooting, November 7, 1938

On the morning of November 7, 1938, Grynszpan bought a revolver, went to the German embassy in Paris, and asked to see an official. He was shown to Third Secretary Ernst vom Rath, and shot him — five shots, two taking effect, in the New York Times's words the next morning. He was arrested on the spot and said he had acted to avenge the persecuted Jews. Vom Rath died on November 9, and that night the regime launched Kristallnacht in his name.

What became of him

Grynszpan was held in French custody awaiting trial. After France fell, he was handed over to the Gestapo in 1940. The regime planned a show trial and repeatedly postponed it. His fate after 1942 is genuinely unknown: no reliable record of his death has surfaced. He was declared dead in 1960. A 2016 claim that he survived the war, based on a photograph identification, remains disputed.
