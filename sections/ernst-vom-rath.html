Ernst vom Rath

The German diplomat shot in Paris on November 7, 1938, whose death two days later was used as the pretext for Kristallnacht.

Ernst vom Rath (1909–1938), Legation Secretary at the German embassy in Paris. Source: Wikimedia Commons

1909–1938

The diplomat

Ernst Eduard vom Rath was born in 1909 in Frankfurt am Main. He joined the German diplomatic service and was posted to Paris as a Legation Secretary (Third Secretary) at the German embassy.

The shooting and his death

On November 7, 1938, Herschel Grynszpan came to the embassy asking to see an official and was shown to vom Rath, the first official available. Grynszpan shot him five times; two bullets took effect. Vom Rath died of his wounds on November 9, 1938. Hitler had sent his own physician to Paris; it made no difference.

How his death was used

The regime staged vom Rath as a martyr: he was given a state funeral in Düsseldorf, attended by Hitler. On the night of his death, Goebbels — with Hitler's approval — used the shooting to unleash the nationwide pogrom that became known as Kristallnacht. Vom Rath himself, a junior diplomat, had no part in choosing any of this; his death was the pretext, not the cause — the cause was the regime's own policy.
