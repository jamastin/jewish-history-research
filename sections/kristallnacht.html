Kristallnacht — The November Pogrom, 1938

The night of November 9–10, 1938: synagogues burned, Jewish shops smashed, and some 30,000 Jewish men sent to concentration camps across Germany and Austria — set off, as the pretext, by a teenager's shots in Paris.

The Börnerplatz synagogue in Frankfurt am Main burning on the morning of November 10, 1938. Source: Wikimedia Commons

Research in progress — contemporary newspaper coverage is being gathered

The pretext — a shooting in Paris

On November 7, 1938, Herschel Grynszpan, a 17-year-old Polish-Jewish refugee in Paris whose parents had just been expelled from Germany, went to the German embassy and shot the diplomat Ernst vom Rath. Vom Rath died of his wounds on November 9. That night, the Nazi leadership used the shooting as the pretext for the pogrom.

The pogrom, November 9–10, 1938

Through the night of November 9–10, SA men and party mobs, with the police ordered to stand back, attacked Jews across the whole Reich — Germany, annexed Austria, and the Sudetenland. Synagogues were set on fire in town after town; the count usually given is more than 1,400 synagogues and prayer houses damaged or destroyed, and about 7,500 Jewish businesses smashed and looted. At least 91 Jews were murdered in the official count; the true number was higher once suicides and deaths in custody are counted. Some 30,000 Jewish men were arrested and sent to Dachau, Buchenwald, and Sachsenhausen; most were released over the following months on condition that they emigrate. The Jewish community was then fined one billion marks for the damage.

The day after: a smashed Jewish shopfront in Berlin, November 1938. Source: Wikimedia Commons

How it got its name

"Kristallnacht" means "crystal night" — in English, the Night of Broken Glass — from the streets covered in glass from the smashed shop windows. The word was used mockingly at the time; many historians and Jewish organizations now prefer to call it the November Pogrom of 1938.

In their own words — what the papers said

"Reich Embassy Aids in Paris Shot To Avenge Expulsions by the Nazis" — "17-Year-Old Polish Jew Fires Five Shots at Third Secretary, Two Taking Effect"

— The New York Times, front page, November 8, 1938 (on the shooting)

"The justifiable and understandable indignation of the German people over the cowardly Jewish murder of a German diplomat in Paris has resulted [last] night in extensive demonstrations" — "The final answer to Jewry will be given in the form of laws or decrees."

— Joseph Goebbels, quoted in the Associated Press dispatch of November 10, 1938; the same dispatch reported "the total or partial destruction of all the 21 synagogs in the city" of Vienna

"Nazi Germany indulged today in the greatest orgy of anti-Jewish violence since Chancellor Hitler came to power in 1933."

— Honolulu Star-Bulletin, November 10, 1938, under the headline "NAZIS START JEW-BAITING"
