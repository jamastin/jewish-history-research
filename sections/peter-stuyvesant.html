Peter Stuyvesant

The governor of New Amsterdam who asked to expel the 23 Jewish refugees of 1654 — and was overruled by his own company.

Petrus (Peter) Stuyvesant, portrait of about 1660. Source: Wikimedia Commons

The 23 arrive

In September 1654, twenty-three Jews — men, women, and children — reached New Amsterdam from Recife, Brazil, after the Portuguese retook Recife and the refugees were robbed on the way north. Governor Peter Stuyvesant asked to expel them. The Dutch West India Company, his employer, overruled him in April 1655 and let them stay — the beginning of the first Jewish community in North America. One more Jewish arrival, Jacob Barsimson, had reached the colony separately, weeks earlier. The full story, with the 1933 Evening Star page that retells it, is on the New Amsterdam, 1654 page.
