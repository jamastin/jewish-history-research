The Roosevelts — "Of Jewish Descent"?

Both presidents were claimed, in print, to be Jews who had changed their name. Here is what was claimed, in the papers' own words — and what the documented record shows. Researched 2026-11-22.

Theodore Roosevelt — the police commissioner

James remembered "something about him being like sheriff or some in a Jewish neighborhood, or some kind of police officer." The documented job behind that memory: Theodore Roosevelt was New York City Police Commissioner, 1895–1897 — president of the Police Commission. In his own Autobiography (1913):

"In the spring of 1895 I was appointed by Mayor Strong Police Commissioner, and I served as President of the Police Commission of New York for the two following years."

While commissioner, the antisemitic Berlin preacher Rector Ahlwardt came to New York, and the city's Jews asked Roosevelt to stop him speaking. Roosevelt refused — and assigned the protection on purpose:

"Accordingly I detailed for his protection a Jew sergeant and a score or two of Jew policemen. He made his harangue against the Jews under the active protection of some forty policemen, every one of them a Jew! It was the most effective possible answer…"

Theodore Roosevelt, An Autobiography (1913), Ch. VI

The Spanish-American War, 1898 — and the "revenge" paper

Jewish soldiers served in Roosevelt's Rough Riders, and in 1900 the Hebrew Veterans of the War with Spain made him an honorary member. "Revenge on Spain" for 1492 was a real theme of the era's Jewish writing — Louis Fine of Key West called his arms-running for Cuba, in his approved 1918 biography, "a part of Jewish vengeance against Spain, the old persecutor of the race."

The specific item James remembers — a Yiddish paper of the war era saying Roosevelt himself "got revenge for them" — has NOT been found yet. Both archives that hold 1898 Jewish papers (Chronicling America and the Historical Jewish Press) blocked the search on 2026-11-22, so the hunt is queued, not closed. Nothing is claimed from pages that could not be opened. One live lead: James's own saved bookmark, the Yakima Herald of May 26, 1898, page 7, where his search already found "Roosevelt" near a Jewish name.

Franklin D. Roosevelt — the "Rosenfeld" claim

The claim's origin is documented: in 1934, former Michigan governor Chase S. Osborn said the Roosevelts descend from a Spanish Jewish family named Rossacampo, expelled from Spain in 1620, the name "variously changed… to Rosenthal, Rosenblum, Rosenbaum, Rosenburg and Rosenvelt." Nazi propagandists then took to calling the president "Rosenfeld" — a name pinned on him by enemies; no period source shows the family ever using it.

FDR answered the story himself, in a letter of March 7, 1935 to Philip Slomovitz, editor of the Detroit Jewish Chronicle:

"All I know about the origin of the Roosevelt family in this country is that all branches bearing the name are apparently descended from Claes Martenssen Van Roosevelt, who came from Holland sometime before 1648… In the dim distant past they may have been Jews or Catholics or Protestants. What I am more interested in is whether they were good citizens and believers in God."

Franklin D. Roosevelt to Philip Slomovitz, March 7, 1935

The record: the ancestor is Claes Martenszen van Rosenvelt of Tholen, Zeeland — "Rosevelt" (rose field) is a Dutch place-name documented since 1481 — and five of his children were baptized in the Reformed Dutch Church in New Amsterdam, 1650–1658. No record connects the family to Judaism; what is documented is that FDR appointed more Jews to high office than any earlier president, which fed the talk.

M152 identified — one page behind both memories

The quote on James's photo — "President Roosevelt is of Jewish descent, though not of the orthodox faith…" — appears word for word in the Polk County Observer (Monmouth, Oregon), Friday, June 5, 1914, page 6, in a syndicated feature, "As Paris Sees Us," quoting a French paper's error-filled sketch of America. The same passage holds the second memory too:

"President Roosevelt is of Jewish descent, though not of the orthodox faith, his people having been converted to the Puritan and Pilgrim faith soon after they emigrated to the New World. Mr. Roosevelt was a policeman for many years before he entered political life…"

Polk County Observer, June 5, 1914, p. 6

The Roosevelt meant is Theodore — the "policeman" is the Police Commissioner of 1895–97, shrunk by the Paris writer. One page explains both halves of what James remembered.
